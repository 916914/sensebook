The story

Knowledge belongs to everyone.
Understanding should too.

জ্ঞান সবার। বোঝার সুযোগও সবার।

Two students can read the same textbook and get two different educations. One can ask a tutor whenever something is hard. The other is alone with the page. For a blind reader, even reaching the printed words is a barrier.

The knowledge is the same. The chance to understand it is not. SenseBook exists to give every reader someone to learn with.

Positioning on a page

The mission is wide. The first promise is narrow.

Who uses it

Everyone who reads, on one identical device. First: Class 8–10 students preparing for the SSC, and blind and low-vision students.

Who pays

Personal: families, starting with the top 10–20% of urban households.
Commons: schools, libraries, centres.
Access Fund: CSR, foundations, NGOs.

The problem

Access to understanding is unequal: 49.8% of children aged 7–14 lack foundational reading, 60.8% foundational numeracy (MICS 2025). 76.5% of secondary students have tutors (Education Watch 2023).

Main alternative

The phone already in the house, plus the guidebook. For blind readers: free talking textbooks, free apps and people.

Why us, not the phone

The book stays the screen: no phone in the child's hands, hands-free page following, your own textbook from verified text even offline, nothing to aim.

Launch promise

Turn the page of your own textbook. SenseBook reads it aloud, explains the hard parts, and quizzes you on it, hands-free.

Brand idea

Someone to learn with.

শেখার একজন সঙ্গী।

SenseBook sits at the top of the page, like a patient friend reading over your shoulder. It never takes the book away; it helps you understand it.

One product. Different lives.

The Class 9 student

Studying physics at 10 pm. No tutor for every subject. She turns the page; SenseBook reads, explains, and quizzes her.

The blind student

The same textbook as her classmates, on the same device, with the same features. Nothing to aim, everything by touch and sound.

The university student

Reading a hard chapter and wanting to talk it through, without switching between five tabs.

The lifelong learner

Out of school, still curious: economics, English, a manual for a new machine.

Illustrative people, not customers or testimonials. The common thread is intellectual independence, never disability, poverty or charity.

Voice

Plain, warm, exact.

Short sentences. One idea each. Concrete words: the page, the chapter, the button.

Say what we know. Mark estimates as estimates. Say "in testing" when it is.

Bangla first in Bangladesh. Standard colloquial (চলিত). তুমি to students, আপনি to parents, adults and institutions. Bangla numerals in Bangla text.

Two voices on the device. The reader voice reads the page; a chime, then the explainer voice helps. The AI never speaks in the book's voice.

Never: revolutionary, magic, smart, genius, never wrong, replaces, the only.

Logo direction · working mark

An open book, and someone at the top of the page.

The pages are any printed book. The gap is the spine. The marigold dot is SenseBook, sitting exactly where the device sits, and the colour of its Ask button: the voice.

It reads as a person with a book, a reader. It works at 16 px, in one colour, and as a raised mark a hand can feel.

The wordmark is Literata SemiBold, outlined. Bangla: Noto Serif Bengali Bold, outlined.

Status: working mark for drafts. The founders will confirm or replace it.

Using the logo

Colour on paper · reversed on Spine · one colour (Ink) for print and fax · on Marigold, ink with a paper dot.

Clear space and size

Keep clear space of one dot diameter on every side. Smallest sizes: mark 16 px or 5 mm; lockup 96 px or 25 mm wide.

Don't

× Don't stretch

× Don't rotate

× No glows or effects

× Don't recolour

Also: no gradients, no sparkles, never on a busy photo without a solid panel, never the device front.

Photography and renders

Brand illustration of SenseBook on a textbook spread. An illustration, labelled as one: not a photo, not a measured render.

Real, warm, ordinary.

Do real Bangladeshi homes, classrooms and libraries; evening table-lamp and window light; real hands, real books, the device at true size.

Do show every kind of reader as capable: a Class 9 student, a university student, a teacher, an older learner, a blind reader in control. Photograph with consent, and with blind readers, not about them.

Do label renders as renders and match them to the CAD dimensions.

Don't use AI-generated people or scenes as if they were real; glowing holograms, neon, purple gradients, floating screens; staged pity; foreign stock classrooms; readable NCTB covers used as endorsement.

Accessibility is the identity

Built in, like a kerb ramp: good for everyone.

Every control by touch

Ask, back, play, forward and mic-mute are shapes a hand can tell apart. Nothing depends on sight.

Every state has a sound

Short, distinct sounds and a spoken line, plus a vibration when the speaker is off.

Two voices

The reader voice reads the book; a chime, then the explainer voice helps.

High contrast

Body text AAA (7:1+). Nothing important is shown by colour alone.

Plain words, two languages

Bangla first in Bangladesh. Short sentences. Every guide in large print, braille and audio.

The same product for everyone

No separate edition, no proof of disability, no 'accessibility mode' to find.

Soft wood and one bell.

The startup sound is the sonic logo: three rising notes, like a page opening. Everything else is shorter. Sounds are never melodies or jingles, and can be turned down separately from speech.

The single bell always comes before the explainer voice, so the AI never sounds like the book.

Brand architecture

One master brand, three ways to reach a reader.

Personal

Your own SenseBook.

Who pays: Readers and families

Commons

Shared SenseBooks in schools, libraries and learning centres.

Who pays: Institutions

Access Fund

Funded access: device, training, maintenance and service.

Who pays: CSR, foundations, NGOs, donors

Rules

Every route gets the same device, the same software and the same look. No "charity edition", no different colour, no label on the device. Programme names are set in the text typeface after the logo, never in a separate logo.

SenseBook Impact Report

Published every year, independently evaluated wherever possible. It reports only what was measured: learners reached, hours, reading independence, and comprehension from independent studies.
