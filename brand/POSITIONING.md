Version 2, 27 November 2026. It joins three inputs:
- the founders' brand direction (the story, purpose and one-product-for-everyone model);
- the validation evidence in [`docs/validation/positioning-inputs.md`](../docs/validation/positioning-inputs.md) (v1.2) and the [validation report](../docs/validation/SenseBook_Validation_Report.md);

Nobody has been interviewed yet and no SenseBook hardware has been tested. Every figure below has a source; estimates are labelled.

**Our product is SenseBook. Our business is personal learning. Our mission is to democratise understanding.**

**The mission is wide and the first promise is narrow.** We start where the need is clearest and the design is strongest: a secondary student's own printed textbook, read from verified text, explained and quizzed, hands-free. We widen the promise only as each capability passes its test.

## 1. Target customer

**Who uses it: everyone who reads.** It is one device with one set of features for every reader, from a Class 8 student to a university student, a teacher or a lifelong learner. A blind or low-vision reader buys, receives and uses exactly the same SenseBook. Accessibility is built in, the way a kerb ramp helps the wheelchair user, the parent with a pram and the traveller with a suitcase. It is never a separate edition.

**Who buys it first, and where we start**

| Route | Buyer | First user | Why them first (evidence) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SenseBook Personal** | A parent in roughly the **top 10–20% of urban households** who already pays for tutoring | Their **Class 8–10 child** preparing for the SSC exam | The device is about 11% of a top-decile monthly income, but about 33% of an average urban one (HIES 2022, validation estimate). 76.5% of secondary students have tutors or coaching (Education Watch 2023). |
| **SenseBook Commons** | Schools, libraries, learning centres and programmes that buy or lease shared devices | Students who can't own one, on scheduled shared access | Shared access is how the mission reaches families who can't pay Tk 14,990. No school purchasing evidence yet (validation: unsupported), so this route is tested before it is planned for. |
| **SenseBook Access Fund** | CSR teams, NGOs, foundations and donors who fund devices, training and service for learners who need them | Blind and low-vision students first (about 35,000–40,000 blind children), then other underserved learners | Families of blind students can't be the buyer: Tk 16,990 is about 17 months of the disability allowance. Bank CSR to education was Tk 64 crore in Jul–Dec 2025. No Bangladeshi sponsor has yet paid for a print-reading device. |

University students, professionals and lifelong learners belong in the brand's imagery and tone from day one: SenseBook is not a children's gadget. They become a sales focus once reading of unfamiliar print and small type passes its test.

## 2. The problem

**The national problem: access to understanding is unequal.** Bangladesh has put textbooks in almost every child's hands. Understanding them is another matter:
- **49.8%** of children aged 7–14 could not show foundational reading skills, and **60.8%** could not show foundational numeracy (MICS 2025, BBS and UNICEF).
- **76.5%** of secondary students had private tutors or coaching in 2023 (Education Watch, CAMPE). Families spent Tk 27,340 a year per Class 9 student in 2022.
- The World Bank approved **$300 million** in November 2026 to improve learning in primary schools, because access is near-universal but learning lags.

Two students can read the same textbook and get two different educations. One can ask a tutor. The other is alone with the page. For a blind reader, even reaching the printed words is a barrier.

**In their words (hypotheses, not quotes).** Real interview words from the [validation kit](../docs/validation/kit/) will replace these:
- **Student:** stuck on one paragraph or diagram late at night, with nobody to ask; the guidebook gives an answer to copy, not an explanation.
- **Parent:** pays for tutors and guidebooks and still can't tell whether the child understands; worries that a phone means distraction and that AI will do the homework.
- **Blind or low-vision reader:** talking textbooks cover the core books, but not guidebooks, notes, revised editions or library books; phone apps need a steady hand to aim, and often a sighted helper.
- **Everyone:** power cuts and weak internet hit exactly when students study.

## 3. The category we compete in

**Personal learning technology: a book-aware learning companion** (বই-সচেতন শেখার সঙ্গী).

| We are not | We are |
|---|---|
| A talking book or talking pen | A learning companion that reads, explains and quizzes |
| A children's gadget | Personal learning hardware for every age |
| A device for blind people | One product, accessible to everyone by design |
| An AI chatbot or another app | A physical, context-aware companion: the book stays the screen |
| A replacement for teachers or tutors | Help on the page you're reading, any evening, between lessons |
| A charity | A commercially sustainable company with a social mission |

We choose this category so that we fight where we are strong: the printed page, no screen in the hands, no aiming, verified textbook text offline, touch and sound. We avoid the fight we would lose, raw AI capability against a free app on the phone the family already owns.

## 4. The main alternative

- **For families: the phone or tablet already in the house, plus the guidebook.**
  - 75.1% of households have a smartphone (BBS 2026).
  - Gemini Live and NotebookLM speak Bengali and cost little or nothing, and every textbook is a free PDF.
  - 93% of secondary students use commercial guidebooks.
  - The closest direct rival is Shikho's EduTab, a Walton tablet with AI support.
  - The private tutor is the price anchor, not the thing we replace.
- **For blind and low-vision readers: free talking textbooks, free apps and people.**
  - DAISY textbooks for Classes 1–10 (a2i and YPSA) are free.
  - Google Lookout and the Envision app are free and list Bengali.
  - Sighted family members read the rest.

**Our answer to "why not just use the phone?"**: the book stays the screen. There is no phone in the child's hands. Page following is hands-free. Your own textbook is read from verified text and cached audio, even offline. For blind readers there is nothing to aim. We do not claim better AI: a phone can use the same models.

## 5. The promise

**Brand promise:** *Never get stuck learning alone again.* · পড়তে গিয়ে আর একা আটকে থাকতে হবে না।

It promises company, someone to learn with at any hour, not results. It must never be read as "you will always understand" or "your grades will rise".

**What it means on launch day, and what we can stand behind:**

> **Turn the page of your own textbook. SenseBook reads it aloud, explains the hard parts, and quizzes you on it, hands-free.**
>
> **নিজের পাঠ্যবইয়ের পাতা ওল্টাও। সেন্সবুক পড়ে শোনায়, কঠিন জায়গা বুঝিয়ে দেয়, আর সেখান থেকেই পড়া ধরে। হাত খালি রেখেই।**

The two voices are part of the promise: *the reader voice reads the page; the explainer voice helps.* Whatever the AI adds always sounds different from the book.

## 7. Proof we still need

The validation report's verdict is: **change, then test; don't tool hardware yet.** Nothing below is proven.

| Proof | Pass means | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Families prefer SenseBook to a phone on a stand | ≥60% keep SenseBook; ≥30% place a deposit at Tk 14,990 | The phone test (2 weeks) |
| It reads Bangla accurately at its real camera view, and understands children's spoken questions | ≤2% character error, ≤1 invented word per 1,000; voice ≥3.5/5 | The Bangla reading test (1–2 weeks) |
| Blind and low-vision readers attach it and read alone; funders commit | ≥80% attach in ≤15 s and read alone; ≥2 letters of intent | The blind-reader and sponsor test (4–6 weeks) |
| Understanding improves against a comparison group | effect size ≥0.2 | Independent pilot study |
| Students keep using it | ≥40% use it 3+ days a week at week 6 | Pilot |
| Shared Commons devices actually serve many learners | learners and hours per device, measured | Commons pilot |

Before any child uses a pilot, the AI provider must allow under-18 use. Google's Gemini API terms currently don't.

## 8. Claims we must never make

Until a test measures it, and then only as measured:

1. **Reading any print:** no "reads any book, letter or newspaper". Say: "reads your textbooks; other print is in testing".
2. **Never wrong:** no "never makes things up" and no "100% accurate". Say: "reads verified text for your textbook, and tells you when it isn't sure".
3. **Conversation quality:** no "understands your questions in Bangla" as a promise. Spoken Bangla is the weakest-tested part.
4. **Grades and savings:** no grade or score gains, and no "saves you tutor money", until the pilot study reports.
5. **Replacing people:** no "replaces your tutor" or "replaces your teacher".
6. **Offline:** no unqualified "works offline". Say: "reads downloaded textbooks without internet".
7. **Privacy:** no "nothing leaves the device". Say: "the mic only listens while you hold Ask; a light shows when anything is sent".
8. **Endorsements:** no NCTB, ministry, school, NGO or UN endorsement or partnership until it is signed. Don't use NCTB's name or logo as an endorsement. Say "works with your school textbooks" only after NCTB permission.
9. **AI provider:** don't name it in brand materials.
10. **Blind readers:** no "blind students can't read their textbooks", because free talking textbooks exist. No "the only Bangla reader". No "built with blind students" until co-design has happened.
11. **Comparisons:** no "one-sixth to one-quarter of the price". The real gap is about 1/14 to 1/40; give real figures. No untested "better than" claims.
12. **Status:** no battery hours, "certified", "safe for children" or "Made in Bangladesh" until true. It is a prototype.
13. **Invented evidence:** no invented users, testimonials, quotes or counts. No AI-generated people shown as real users. No illustrative data that looks like real data.
14. **Pity or stigma:** no portraying anyone as a charity case. Beneficiaries get the same product, never a "charity edition".

## 9. Positioning statements

**One line**

> SenseBook is personal learning technology for everyone who reads: a screen-free companion that clips onto a printed book, reads the page aloud, explains it and quizzes you on it, in Bangla and English, and works the same for blind and sighted readers.

> সেন্সবুক যাঁরা পড়েন তাঁদের সবার জন্য ব্যক্তিগত শেখার প্রযুক্তি: পর্দাহীন এক সঙ্গী, যা ছাপা বইয়ে আটকে বসে পাতা পড়ে শোনায়, বুঝিয়ে দেয় আর পড়া ধরে, বাংলা ও ইংরেজিতে; দৃষ্টিহীন ও দৃষ্টিসম্পন্ন পাঠকের জন্য একই রকম।

**One paragraph**

> Two students can read the same textbook and get two different educations: one can ask a tutor, the other is alone with the page. SenseBook exists to close that gap. It is a small, screen-free companion that clips onto a printed book. Turn the page and it follows: it reads the page aloud from verified text, explains what is hard in Bangla or English, and quizzes you so you remember. The reader voice and the explainer voice always sound different, so you know what the book says and what the AI adds. Every reader gets the same device: a blind student and a university student buy, receive and use exactly the same SenseBook, and every function works by touch and sound. We begin with Bangladesh's secondary textbooks, where the need is clearest, and widen as each capability is proven.

> দুজন শিক্ষার্থী একই পাঠ্যবই পড়েও দুই রকম শিক্ষা পায়: একজন গৃহশিক্ষককে প্রশ্ন করতে পারে, আরেকজন পাতার সামনে একা। সেন্সবুক এই ফাঁক কমাতে চায়। এটি ছোট, পর্দাহীন এক সঙ্গী, যা ছাপা বইয়ে আটকে বসে। পাতা ওল্টালে সে-ও সঙ্গে চলে: যাচাই করা লেখা থেকে পাতা পড়ে শোনায়, কঠিন জায়গা বাংলা বা ইংরেজিতে বুঝিয়ে দেয়, আর পড়া ধরে, যাতে মনে থাকে। পাঠের কণ্ঠ আর ব্যাখ্যার কণ্ঠ সবসময় আলাদা শোনায়, তাই বোঝা যায় কোনটা বইয়ের কথা আর কোনটা এআই-এর যোগ করা। প্রত্যেক পাঠক পান একই যন্ত্র: একজন দৃষ্টিহীন শিক্ষার্থী আর একজন বিশ্ববিদ্যালয়ের ছাত্র ঠিক একই সেন্সবুক কেনেন, পান আর ব্যবহার করেন, আর প্রতিটি কাজ স্পর্শ ও শব্দ দিয়েই করা যায়। আমরা শুরু করছি বাংলাদেশের মাধ্যমিক পাঠ্যবই দিয়ে, যেখানে প্রয়োজন সবচেয়ে স্পষ্ট, আর প্রতিটি সক্ষমতা প্রমাণিত হলে পরিসর বাড়াব।

## 11. Brand architecture

One master brand, three routes. Never three identities.

| Name | What it is | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| **SenseBook** | The device, its intelligence and its brand. Identical for every reader. | — |
| **SenseBook Personal** | Owning a SenseBook | Readers and families |
| **SenseBook Commons** | Shared SenseBooks in schools, libraries and learning centres | Institutions (purchase or lease, plus service) |
| **SenseBook Access Fund** | Funded access for learners who need it, covering the device, training, maintenance and service | CSR, foundations, NGOs, donors |

Plus one public commitment: the **SenseBook Impact Report**, published every year and independently evaluated wherever possible. It reports only what was measured.

## 12. Theory of change

| Step | What |
|---|---|
| **Problem** | Unequal access to personal guidance while learning |
| **What we do** | Universally accessible, book-aware learning companions, owned, shared and funded |
| **Immediate outcome** | More chances to ask, hear and study independently, on the page in front of you |
| **Intermediate outcome** | Better comprehension and retention, and more confidence, *if the pilot study shows it* |
| **Long-term impact** | Greater educational equality, independent thinking and national capability |
