# SenseBook: final attachment concept v4, the "tri-grip" (founder's idea)

## The idea
Under the unit, the slim stick ends in a small hub with **three very thin legs, like a miniature tripod**:
- **Two front legs:** wafer-thin flat blades (spring steel, about 0.4 mm thick, 8 mm wide, rounded and coated). They slide down **inside the front and back covers**, one each side of the spine, between each cover and its pages.
- **Rear leg:** reaches behind the book and **presses gently on the back of the spine** with a soft pad.
- **Hub spring:** a torsion spring keeps a light, even grip, so the legs fit any thickness.

The book's covers and spine are held between the blades in front and the rear leg behind. The unit sits on the stick above the hub, **exactly centred over the spine**, just behind the top edge, as in the PDF.

## Why it works
| Need | How the tri-grip meets it |
|---|---|
| PDF look | Symmetric: the hub sits on the spine, so the unit is centred automatically |
| Any book | Hardcover, paperback, thin booklet, workbook, spiral (the rear leg rests on the coil), dictionary, dust jacket. The hub spring adapts from a few millimetres to about 6 cm |
| Turning pages | The blades lie under the pages, inside the covers, and the legs stay behind the top edge. A turning page, even straight up, passes in front of everything |
| In the hand | Three-point grip on the book; the device moves with the book, so the view never changes |
| Table | Book open on the table: the blades are pinned by the pages' weight and the rear leg rests on the table behind, a true tripod |
| No book (letters, sheets) | Spread the three legs and it stands on the table by itself as a mini tripod |
| Portable, minimal | Legs fold up along the short stick like a camera tripod. Stowed it is a small baton (about 10 × 4 × 3.5 cm including the unit) |
| Safe | No magnets; no strong pinch; smooth rounded blades that don't mark paper |

## Stand height: as short as the camera allows
The lowest height at which the camera still sees a whole school-textbook spread (about 37 × 24.5 cm) depends on the lens:

| Lens | Lowest lens height | Stick length | Far edge of the page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camera Module 3 Wide (102°) | 15 cm | about 13–14 cm | Squashed to about half (readable) |
| **Ultra-wide 120° (same 12 MP sensor, M12 lens)** | **about 11 cm** | **about 9 cm** | Squashed to about 40%: fine for recognising textbook pages, weaker for small unfamiliar print |
| Fisheye 140° | about 7 cm | about 5–6 cm | Squashed to about a quarter, with heavy distortion: too blurry to read text. Not recommended |

**Decision proposed:** the ultra-wide lens with a **9 cm telescoping stick**.
- It stays short (about 9 cm) for everyday textbook reading. Those pages are recognised from the library, so the squash doesn't matter.
- It pulls up to about 18 cm for small print in unfamiliar books, where the view is sharper.
- Software straightens the image (lens and perspective correction).
- The ultra-wide M12 lens for the 12 MP sensor still needs a supplier sample and a bench check.

## The unit
- About 8.5 × 3.8 × 3 cm, four status dots on the upright front, Ask button on top. Inside are the camera, mics, speaker, headphone jack, computer and battery.
- **The lens looks DOWN at the pages:** it sits on a sloped facet at the bottom of the front, with the camera inside angled about 50–60° below horizontal.
- A tilt joint fine-tunes the angle.

## How anyone attaches it, blind readers included
1. Close the book with the spine on the left.
2. Squeeze the hub: the legs open.
3. Slide one blade under the front cover and the other under the back cover at the top edge beside the spine (lift each cover corner slightly). The rear leg goes behind the spine.
4. Let go: the spring closes the grip with a soft click. A sensor in the hub detects it; the device says "Attached" and buzzes.
5. Open the book to your page. The blade hinges turn with the covers, and the device says "Ready, I can see pages 42 and 43" or gives a correction ("book upside down", "move the book a little away").
6. To remove it, squeeze the hub and lift.

All of this works by touch: the spine is easy to find, the blades have a raised dot near the hub, and there is nothing to align by eye.

## Honest open points (to test, not assume)
- **Blade grip in a shaking hand** (bus, walking): blade surface texture and spring force need tuning.
- **Very round or very thick spines:** the rear-leg pad shape needs testing; 6 cm of travel may not cover the biggest dictionaries.
- **Flush paperbacks:** the blade's bend at the top edge sits right beside the page corners near the spine; check that pages don't catch.
- **Blind attachment:** 3 blind testers × 10 books, attached alone in under 15 seconds.
- **Camera:** small unfamiliar print at the reader-side edge is less sharp at this height; textbooks are unaffected (recognised from the library).

## Bench test before freezing (1 week, about Tk 2,000)
3D-printed hub, spring-steel strip blades (shim stock), off-the-shelf torsion spring. Try 30 real books, plus in-hand, page-turn and table-tripod trials.
