# SenseBook: universal for every kind of book (proposal v2, for approval)

## The concept, in one line
A webcam-sized unit sits at the top of an open book, centred on the spine, exactly like the PDF. It reads, explains, discusses, tests and reminds. It works on a table and in the hand, the pages turn freely, and it works with **every** kind of book.

## Why a clip can't be universal
A clip needs a firm cover to grip:
- thin paper covers bend;
- photocopies and loose sheets have no cover;
- dust jackets slip.
Anything that grips the book depends on how that book was made.

## What is universal: the book rests on the device
The stick rises from a **thin fold-out reading board** that is part of the device. The book, or anything else to be read, rests on the board. Because nothing grips the book, it doesn't matter what the book is.

### The parts
1. **Unit** (the PDF look, webcam-sized, about 8.5 × 3.8 × 3 cm). It holds:
   - the camera, microphones and speaker;
   - the Ask button and four status dots;
   - the headphone jack.
2. **Stick**, about 15–20 cm, rising from the top centre of the board, with a tilt joint under the unit.
3. **Reading board**, 3 mm thick:
   - **Size:** opens to about 26 × 20 cm; folds in half.
   - **Surface:** grippy silicone, so the book doesn't slide.
   - **Top stop:** a soft raised edge at the top. The book's top edge rests against it, which lines up every book in the same place in front of the camera, whatever its size. That also helps a blind reader find the right position by touch.
   - **In-hand band:** a stretchy band at one corner, like a planner band, holds the book to the board when it is lifted into the hands.
   - **Battery:** a thin flat battery and the computer sit inside the board, under the stick. That keeps the unit tiny and the weight low, where it is steady.

### Every case
| Situation | How it works |
|---|---|
| Thick textbook or guidebook on a table | The book lies on the board, top edge against the stop. The unit looks down from behind the top edge. |
| Thin booklet, workbook, magazine | Same; the board gives it a firm back. |
| Photocopies, loose sheets, letters | Lay them on the board; the top stop lines them up. |
| Spiral-bound or a notebook | Lay it on the board open or folded back. |
| Big hardcover or dictionary | It rests on the board and overhangs the sides, which is fine. |
| In the hands (chair, sofa, bus) | Lift the book with the board behind it, held by the band. The device moves with the book, so the view never changes. |
| Lying in bed | Hold the board and book up together; listen through earphones. |
| Turning pages | Nothing touches the pages, and the unit sits behind the top edge. Reading pauses for the hand and continues on the new page (already in the software). |
| Carrying | The board folds in half around the folded stick and unit: about 20 × 13 × 3.5 cm, a slim pencil case. |

## Honest trade-offs
- **Bigger to carry than a clip:** pencil-case size, not pocket size.
- **Heavier in the hand:** about 180–200 g in total, roughly one phone.
- **Holding style:** in the hand you hold the board and book together. That is easy for one-hand reading and floppy paperbacks, and we need to test it with students over 30 minutes.
- **Sharpness:** small unfamiliar print at the reader-side edge stays less sharp at this height (see the earlier estimates). School textbooks are unaffected because they are recognised from the library.

## Test before building
- One week with cardboard and 3D-printed boards: 30 real books and papers (NCTB Class 6–10, guidebooks, booklets, photocopies, spiral, hardcover).
- 30-minute in-hand sessions with 5 students and 3 blind readers.
- Score: slipping, comfort, framing, page turning.
