# SenseBook: final attachment concept (v3, for approval)

## Requirements this must meet, all at once
1. The PDF look: a webcam-sized unit on a short stick at the top of the open book, at the spine.
2. Minimal and portable: folds to about webcam size. No board, no base.
3. Universal: any book, cover or thickness.
4. Works on a table and in the hand: the device moves with the book.
5. Seamless page turning: nothing touches the pages.

## The answer: a magnetic fold-over mount
At the bottom of the stick is a slim mount made of **two thin rounded plates joined by a flexible hinge strip**. It works like the fold-over magnetic bookmarks sold everywhere, scaled up:
- It folds over the top edge of the book's **cover**, beside the spine.
- One plate sits on the outside of the cover. The other slides inside, between the cover and the first page.
- **Magnets in both plates pull together through the cover.**

There are no spring jaws, so:
- the hinge strip simply bends to whatever thickness is between the plates;
- the inner plate lies under all the pages, so turning them never touches it.

### Why this is universal (where a spring clip isn't)
| Book | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Hardcover, thick boards (up to about 5 mm) | Magnet pairs hold through the board |
| Paperback, card cover | Same, with no pinch marks (soft silicone faces) |
| Thin paper cover, workbook, booklet | The inner plate stiffens the cover's top corner, so it doesn't flop |
| Dust jacket | The magnets hold through jacket and board together |
| Spiral-bound, notebook | Folds over the front or back cover |
| Magazine | Folds over the cover |
| Huge dictionary, tiny pocket book | Size doesn't matter; it only uses the top edge beside the spine |
| Loose sheets, photocopies | It can grip the top corner of a stack of sheets the same way |

### The parts
1. **Unit** (the PDF look): about 8.5 × 3.8 × 3 cm, four status dots, one round lens with a soft light ring, marigold Ask button on top. Inside are the camera, 2 mics, speaker, headphone jack, computer and battery.
   - **Camera direction:** the lens must look DOWN at the pages, not straight ahead. It sits on a sloped lower-front facet, a bevel along the bottom of the front face. The camera inside is mounted at the same angle, so its view points about 50–60° below horizontal, at the middle of the open spread.
   - The tilt joint under the unit fine-tunes the angle for big or small books and for in-hand use.
   - The four status dots, microphones and speaker stay on the upright part of the front, facing the reader.
   - Seen from the reader's chair, it still looks like the PDF: dots on top, lens below.
2. **Stick:** slim and telescoping, about 8 cm collapsed and 18 cm extended. It has a tilt joint under the unit and a swivel at the mount, so the unit sits centred over the spine even though the mount is 2–3 cm to one side.
3. **Fold-over mount:** two plates of about 4 × 8 cm, under 1 mm thick each, joined by a silicone hinge strip, with neodymium magnet pairs inside.

### Every scenario
| Scenario | What happens |
|---|---|
| Table | The book lies flat. The mount is on the cover beside the spine, and the unit sits just behind the top edge (the PDF picture). The book's own weight adds grip. |
| In the hand | The device moves with the book, so the camera's view never changes. Total weight is about 90 g, half a phone. |
| Lying in bed | The joints hold their angle; listen through earphones or Bluetooth earbuds. |
| Turning pages | The inner plate lies under every page, and the unit sits behind the top edge. A page standing straight up passes in front of it. Reading pauses for the hand and continues on the new page (already in the software). |
| Changing books | Pull the mount off and fold it onto the next book: one second, one hand. |
| No book (letters, papers) | Fold the mount over the top corner of the paper stack, or open it flat as a small foot. |
| Blind reader | Find the top edge beside the spine by touch, fold the mount over and hear "ready". The device says "page edge not visible" until the view is right. |
| Carrying | The stick collapses and folds under the unit, and the mount plates fold against it: about 9 × 4.5 × 5 cm, webcam size. |

## How a blind reader attaches it (designed for touch alone)
**Rule: attach to the closed book, then open it.** With the book closed, the front cover is simply on top, so there is no hunting for the gap between the cover and the first page.

1. **Hold the device by the unit.** The Ask button on top shows which way is up. The mount hangs below with its plates held open by a light detent, so they can't snap shut in the hand.
2. **Find the corner.** With the book closed and the spine on the left, run a finger to the top-left corner where the top edge meets the spine. Blind readers already find books this way.
3. **Push the mount into that corner until it stops on both sides.**
   - The rounded hinge stops against the top edge, which sets the depth.
   - A small tab on the outer plate stops against the spine, which sets the distance from the spine.
   - It positions itself; nothing needs to be measured.
   - The thin inner plate, marked with a raised dot, slips under the cover's edge as it goes in.
4. **Let go.** The magnets pull the plates shut with a firm click you can feel and hear.
5. **The device confirms:**
   - a sensor in the mount detects the plates closing; the device says "Attached" and gives a short vibration;
   - then, after you open the book, "Ready, I can see pages 42 and 43", or "Book upside down", or "Page edge not visible, move the book a little away".
6. **Open the book to your page.** The stick swivels the unit over the centre and clicks at the centre position.
7. **To remove it,** pull the ridged tab on the outer plate. That peels the magnets apart one edge at a time, so no strength is needed.

**Easier still, a spare mount on each textbook:**
- Spare mounts are about Tk 150 each and stay on each textbook.
- The stick then docks onto a mount with a **self-aligning magnetic snap**, like a phone's magnetic charger: bring it close and it pulls itself into place.
- That is the easiest option for blind readers and for quick book changes.

**Tactile marks:** a raised dot on the inner plate (goes inside the cover), ridges on the pull tab, and a notch on the stick that faces the reader.

**Magnet safety:** magnets are fully sealed inside the plates (a swallowing hazard for small children if loose). There is a warning for pacemaker users to keep it away from the chest, the same advice as for phones with magnetic chargers.

**Must pass with blind co-designers before the design is frozen:**
- 3 blind readers × 10 books each;
- attached alone after one demonstration;
- in under 10 seconds;
- with no wrong-side or upside-down attachments left uncorrected by the voice guidance.

## Numbers to prove on the bench (estimates, not measurements)
- **Holding force:**
  - Need: about 2.5 N per magnet pair to resist the tipping moment of a 90 g device on a 15 cm stick in the hand.
  - Expected: N52 12 × 3 mm magnet pairs give several times that through 4–5 mm.
- **Inner plate:** about 0.8 mm thick. Pages lying over it show a slight bump, which the software ignores.
- **Camera:** the wide lens at about 16–20 cm covers a textbook spread. Small unfamiliar print at the reader-side edge is less sharp; textbooks are unaffected (recognised from the library).

## Bench test before freezing the design (1 week, about Tk 2,000)
- 3D-print the plates and use off-the-shelf N52 magnets.
- Test on 30 real books: NCTB Class 6–10 in both versions, guidebooks, booklets, hardcovers with jackets, spiral, magazines, photocopies.
- Measure: slip, tipping, cover marks, page-turn contact, 30-minute in-hand comfort with 5 students and 3 blind readers.
- Pass: holds on every book, with no slip or page contact.
