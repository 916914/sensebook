SenseBook is personal learning technology: a small, screen-free companion that clips onto a printed book. Turn the page of your own textbook: it reads it aloud, explains the hard parts and quizzes you on it, in Bangla and English, hands-free. A blind student and a university student use exactly the same SenseBook.

Prototype stage, 27 November 2026. Figures are plan estimates unless a source is given.

Why not the phone?

The phone is the real rival: 75.1% of households own one (BBS 2026). SenseBook wins where the phone can't: no screen in the child's hands, nothing to aim, hands-free page following, the student's own textbook from verified text even offline, and every control by touch. We don't claim better AI.

One product, three routes

Personal: families buy (device Tk 14,990, to be tested; subscription Tk 399 a month).
Commons: schools and libraries share devices.
Access Fund: CSR, foundations and NGOs fund device, training and service (plan Tk 16,990 a reader). Same device for everyone.

Evidence so far: "Change, then test. Don't tool hardware yet." Our desk validation (about 390 sources) found a real need and unproven hard parts. Three cheap tests come next: the phone test (do families prefer it and pay a deposit?), the Bangla reading test (accuracy at the real camera view), and the blind-reader and sponsor test (attach and read alone; two letters of intent).

Roadmap

Now: the three tests. To May 2027: co-design and home trials. Jun–Nov 2027: 100-unit pilot with an independent learning study. From Dec 2027: launch, if the gates pass. Every year: a SenseBook Impact Report with only measured results.

What we ask for now

Partners for the tests (schools, blind-reader organisations, 10 families); two sponsor letters of intent for 20+ units; Tk 5–10 lakh non-dilutive for prototypes and tests.
