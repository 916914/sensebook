# Interview guide: Class 6–10 students

**Goal:** learn how students study at home, where they get stuck and what they do about it, before showing anything. **Length:** 30–40 minutes. **Who:** one student, with a guardian nearby. **Language:** the student's choice. Translate this guide into Bangla and have someone back-translate it before use.

**Rules for the interviewer**
- Ask about the **last time** something happened, not "would you". Past behaviour predicts; opinions about the future don't.
- Don't describe SenseBook until Part 5. Don't say "AI device" earlier.
- Write the student's own words. Count things: minutes, taka, days.
- If they say "I'd use it every day", ask "What did you do yesterday evening instead?"

## Part 1. Warm-up (3 min)
1. Which class are you in? Bangla or English version? Which subjects are hardest?
2. Do you study at home, at a tutor's, at coaching or at school after class? Roughly how many hours each on a normal school day?

## Part 2. The last time you got stuck (10 min)
3. Think of the last time you got stuck on something in a textbook at home. What was it? Which subject? What time of day?
4. What did you do next? (Probe: asked someone, phone, guidebook, waited for the tutor, skipped it.) How long did it take to get unstuck, or did you give up?
5. How often does that happen in a normal week? (Count.)
6. Who in your home can help with which subjects?
7. Do you ever read aloud, or listen to someone read, to understand or remember? When did you last do that?

## Part 3. Tutors, coaching and guides (7 min)
8. Do you have a private tutor or go to coaching? For which subjects? How many days a week?
9. What does the tutor do that the textbook doesn't? (Probe: explains, gives notes, sets tests, makes you study, exam suggestions.)
10. Do you use guidebooks (note-boi)? Which? What for?
11. If you know: how much does your family pay each month for tutoring and coaching? (Leave blank if the student doesn't know; ask the parent.)

## Part 4. Phones and apps (7 min)
12. Do you have your own phone, or use a family phone? For how long each day can you use it?
13. Have you used a phone for studying in the last week? What exactly? (Probe: YouTube, 10 Minute School, Shikho, Google Lens, ChatGPT, Gemini, Facebook groups.)
14. Last time you asked ChatGPT, Gemini or a similar app about schoolwork: what did you ask, did you type, speak or take a photo, and was the answer right? In Bangla or English?
15. What stops you using the phone more for studying? (Probe: parents' rules, data cost, distraction, battery, sharing.)
16. When did you last have a power cut or no internet while studying? What did you do?

## Part 5. The idea (8 min)
Read this neutral description, the same way every time:
> "This is an idea for a small device. It clips onto the top of your open textbook. It can read the page aloud, explain a part you don't understand when you ask by voice, ask you quiz questions, and remind you what to revise. It has no screen. It works with earphones."

17. In your own words, what would you use it for? When?
18. Think about last week. Which evening would you have used it, and for what?
19. What would make you stop using it after a week?
20. Compared with asking ChatGPT or Gemini on a phone, what would be better or worse?
21. Would you rather have this, or the same features as an app on your family phone? Why?

(If a prototype or phone-on-stand set-up is available, let the student try one real page and one question now. Note what confuses them, without helping for the first 60 seconds.)

## Part 6. Close (2 min)
22. Is there anything about studying at home that I should have asked and didn't?
23. Can we contact your parent about a one-week trial? (Only with the guardian's consent.)

## After the interview (interviewer, 5 min)
- Times stuck per week; what they did; minutes lost.
- Tutor or coaching: yes or no; subjects; cost if known.
- Phone access: own, shared or none; daily minutes.
- AI use for study in the last week: yes or no; what for.
- Strongest quote (exact words).
- Interest after the description: none, polite, real (gave a specific past evening and use).
