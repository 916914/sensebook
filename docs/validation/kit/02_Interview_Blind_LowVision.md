# Interview guide: blind and low-vision readers

**Goal:** understand how blind and low-vision readers get access to print today, what fails, and whether a clip-on reader would make them more independent. **Length:** 40–50 minutes, including a hands-on task. **Who:** students (with guardian consent) and adults. **Ideally:** led or co-led by a blind or low-vision researcher, paid as an expert.

**Rules**
- The participant is the expert on their own life. Ask about real recent reading, not about "blind people in general".
- Ask before touching or guiding the participant or their things. Describe the room and who is present.
- Offer the consent form in the format they choose (spoken, braille, large print, audio file).
- Record their words and what they actually did in the task, not what they say they would do.

## Part 1. About you (5 min)
1. How would you describe your vision? (Their words. Note: blind / low vision / changing.) Since when?
2. Do you read braille? Large print? Do you use a magnifier, a CCTV magnifier, a phone, a computer, a screen reader?
3. For students: which class, which school (integrated, special, mainstream), Bangla or English version?

## Part 2. Reading today (12 min)
4. Tell me about the last time you needed to read something printed: a textbook page, a letter, a bill, a notice, a medicine label. What was it? What did you do?
5. Who usually reads print for you? How do you feel about asking? What can't you ask them to read?
6. For students: when did you get your textbooks this year, in braille, audio or print? What was missing (maths, science diagrams, tables)?
7. Have you used the free accessible talking textbooks (DAISY or multimedia books) or any audio textbooks? Where did you get them? What worked and what didn't?
8. Have you used a phone app that reads text from the camera (for example Google Lookout, Seeing AI, Envision, Be My Eyes)? Which one? When last? In Bangla or English?
9. What goes wrong with phone apps? (Probe: aiming the camera, framing the whole page, lighting, curved pages, Bangla not supported, data cost, battery, holding the phone and the book together.)
10. What does reading print cost you today, in money, time or depending on others?

## Part 3. Devices you use (5 min)
11. What assistive devices or software have you been given or bought? Who paid? How did you get them? (Probe: government, JPUF, an NGO, a school, family.)
12. What happened to the last device that broke or stopped working? Who fixed it?
13. Do you use wired earphones or Bluetooth earbuds? Do you charge devices yourself every day?

## Part 4. Hands-on task (12 min, only if a prototype, a mock-up or a phone-on-stand set-up is available)
Describe the object in words first. Then hand it over. Don't help for the first 60 seconds unless asked or unless something is unsafe.
- **Task A, attach:** "Please attach this to the top of this closed book, then open the book to any page." Time it. Note each step that confused them. Repeat with a thick book and a thin booklet.
- **Task B, read:** "Ask it to read this page. Then go back one sentence." Note whether they finish alone.
- **Task C, ask:** "Ask a question about the page." Note whether the change from reading voice to explaining voice was clear.
- **Task D, other print:** a letter or a medicine box on the table, in tripod mode.
- After each task: "What was hard? What would make it easier?"

## Part 5. The idea (5 min)
If no prototype, read this neutral description:
> "This is an idea for a small device with a camera. It clips onto the top of an open book, or stands on a table. It reads the page aloud in Bangla or English, can describe pictures and read equations in words, and answers questions about the page by voice. It has tactile buttons and no screen, and works with earphones."

14. What would you read with it first? Where?
15. What would make you stop using it?
16. How does it compare with what you use now (phone app, human reader, talking books)?
17. Who do you think should pay for a device like this for students? For adults?

## Part 6. Close (3 min)
18. What should the designers never get wrong?
19. Would you like to test it again or join the co-design group (paid)?

## After the interview (interviewer)
- Main reading method today; main failure.
- Phone app experience: none, tried, regular; Bangla needs.
- Task results: attach time (seconds) per book, finished alone yes or no; read yes or no; ask yes or no.
- Who paid for current devices.
- Strongest quote.
