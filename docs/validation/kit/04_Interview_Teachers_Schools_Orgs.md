# Interview guide: teachers, schools, organisations and funders

**Goal:** find out who would recommend, buy or fund SenseBook, with what budget, through which process and on what evidence. **Length:** 30–45 minutes. **Who:** teachers and heads in private, government and integrated schools; organisations of and for blind people; NGO education or disability programme staff; CSR and sustainability teams at telecoms and banks.

**Rules**
- Ask how they bought or funded the last similar thing. Processes and budgets are facts; enthusiasm isn't.
- Ask who signs. Find out the budget cycle and the paperwork.
- The real test is a written letter of intent or a pilot commitment, not "this is great".

## A. Teachers and school heads (30 min)
1. How do students in your school study at home? Which classes and subjects need most help?
2. How common is private tutoring among your students? Is coaching linked to the school?
3. What technology does the school use for teaching or study (computer lab, multimedia classroom, tablets, apps)? Who chose it? Who paid? What happened after a year?
4. Do you have students who are blind or have low vision? How do they get their books and read in class and at home?
5. If students used a reading and quiz device at home, what would you want to see as a teacher? (Probe: which chapters, common mistakes, a weekly summary.)
6. What would worry you? (Probe: cheating, wrong answers, children's data, cost to parents.)
7. How does your school decide to buy something for students? Who decides? When is the budget set? What did you last buy for more than Tk 50,000, and how was it chosen?
8. Would the school pay for a reading-corner set of 10 devices, or recommend it to parents, or neither? Under what conditions?
9. Would you host a free two-week pilot with 10 students? What would you need from us?

## B. Organisations of and for blind people (40 min)
1. How many blind and low-vision people do you work with? Students and adults? Where?
2. What do your members use to read print today? What do they ask for most?
3. What do you think of the accessible talking textbooks and braille books provided today? What is missing?
4. Which assistive devices have you distributed? Who funded them? What did they cost? What happened to them after a year (use, breakage, repair, charging)?
5. What has failed before, and why?
6. How do you choose devices? Do your members test them first? Who decides?
7. What would a device like SenseBook have to prove before you'd recommend it?
8. Would you co-design and test it with us, paid? Would you distribute it?
9. What price per reader can your funders cover, including training and support? Over what period?

## C. NGOs, donors and CSR teams (30 min)
1. What does your education or disability programme fund now? What budget per person or per device?
2. Have you funded assistive technology before? What, how much, how many people? How did you measure results?
3. What evidence do you need before funding a new device (pilot data, an independent evaluation, government endorsement, a partner NGO)?
4. What is your budget cycle? When are next year's proposals decided?
5. Do you prefer to fund devices, training, content (accessible books) or research?
6. Could you commit, in writing, to a pilot of 20–40 units if the pilot gates are met? At what price per reader (device, training and two years of service)?

## After each interview (interviewer)
- Organisation type; decision maker; budget cycle and timing.
- Last comparable purchase or grant: what, amount, outcome.
- Evidence they require.
- Commitment level: none / interest / pilot offer / written letter of intent (with units and price).
- Strongest quote.
