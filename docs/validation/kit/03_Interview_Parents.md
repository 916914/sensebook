# Interview guide: parents and guardians

**Goal:** understand what parents already spend on their child's study, what they worry about, how they decide on purchases, and whether they would pay for SenseBook. **Length:** 30 minutes. **Who:** the person who pays for tutoring and devices (ask who that is). **Language:** Bangla unless they prefer English.

**Rules**
- Ask about money already spent, in taka, before talking about SenseBook.
- Never argue with a price reaction. Write it down.
- The real test is a refundable deposit (Part 5), not "would you buy".

## Part 1. The child's study (5 min)
1. Which class is your child in? Bangla or English version? Do they study at home in the evening? With whom?
2. What does your child find hardest? How do you know?
3. When your child gets stuck at home, what happens? Who helps? When did that last happen?

## Part 2. What you spend today (8 min)
4. Does your child have a private tutor or go to coaching? How many subjects, how many days? How much do you pay each month in total?
5. What else do you buy for study: guidebooks, model tests, apps, a phone or tablet, internet data? Roughly how much this year?
6. What was the largest single thing you bought for your child's education in the last two years? How did you pay (cash, bKash, card, EMI)? How did you decide?
7. If you could cut one study cost, which would it be? Why?

## Part 3. Phones, AI and worries (7 min)
8. Does your child use a phone? Own or shared? What rules do you have?
9. What worries you about phones for study? (Probe: games, social media, time, eyes.)
10. Have you or your child used ChatGPT, Gemini or a similar app? For what? What do you think about AI helping with homework?
11. What would make you trust a study helper: a teacher's recommendation, the school, exam results, other parents?

## Part 4. The idea (6 min)
Read the same neutral description as in the student guide, then add:
> "Parents get a weekly report of study time, chapters and weak topics, and can lock a mode that gives hints instead of answers. Learning memory stays off until a parent turns it on."

12. What do you think your child would use it for? How often?
13. What would make you say no to it?
14. How does it compare with a tutor? With a tablet? With a phone app?
15. **Price questions (ask in this order, write the taka figure for each):**
    - At what price would it be so cheap that you'd doubt the quality?
    - At what price would it be a good deal?
    - At what price would it start to feel expensive, though you might still consider it?
    - At what price would it be too expensive to consider?
16. A monthly subscription for unlimited questions, quizzes and reports: what would you pay a month? Would you pay for a year at once?

## Part 5. The deposit test (3 min)
Only if a real pre-order list exists and the deposit is fully refundable. Explain the terms clearly.
> "We are taking a small number of refundable pre-orders for the first batch at Tk [price A / B / C, assigned in advance]. A Tk 500 deposit holds a place and is refunded in full on request at any time. Would you like to place one?"

17. Record: yes, no or "later". If no, ask: "What would need to change?"

Rotate the three prices (for example Tk 9,990, Tk 12,990 and Tk 14,990) so each price is shown to a similar number of parents. Never show two prices to the same parent.

## Part 6. Close (1 min)
18. Who else should we talk to? (Another parent, a teacher.)

## After the interview (interviewer)
- Monthly tutoring and coaching spend (Tk); other study spend this year (Tk).
- Largest education purchase and how paid.
- The four price answers (Tk); the monthly subscription answer (Tk).
- Deposit: price shown; yes, no or later; reason.
- Main worry; main trust source.
