# Class 6–10 students in Bangladesh: study help used today, what it costs families, and device access

*Research date: 27 Nov 2026. Each finding gives publisher and publication date. "Page fetched 27 Nov 2026" means a live product or price page with no publication date. "(snippet)" means the fact came from a search-result summary and the page itself was not opened, so treat it as lower confidence. Tk = Bangladeshi taka. The SenseBook price (Tk 14,990, about US$122) implies about Tk 123 per US$.*

## 1. Private tutoring and coaching: how many students use it, what it costs, and where the "76.5%" and "71%" claims come from

### Takeaway
About three-quarters or more of secondary students use private tutors or coaching. The "76.5% of secondary students have a private tutor" figure is real. It comes from the civil-society Education Watch 2023 study (the CAMPE platform), published March 2024, which sampled Classes 5–9. It is not from BBS, HIES or the World Bank. "Households carry 71% of education costs" is also real. It comes from UNESCO's GEM Report 2022 South Asia edition on non-state actors (2 Nov 2022), launched in Dhaka with BRAC in January 2023. Typical spending on a secondary tutor is about Tk 1,100–3,000 a month nationally (2022). In Dhaka, two tutors or a coaching centre cost Tk 4,000–8,000+ a month.

### Cited Findings
**Where the 76.5% comes from**
- "In 2023, 70 percent primary and 76.5 percent secondary students had private tutors." The report is attributed to "Education Watch", a civil-society research platform. Sample: 2,077 students, 3,129 parents and 2,019 teachers, SMC members and officials across 26 upazilas. Headline: "Education Expenses: Primary sees a 25pc rise, secondary 51", by Mohiuddin Alamgir — [The Daily Star, 30 Mar 2024](https://www.thedailystar.net/node/3578376)
- The same study reported as "Education Watch-2023": "more than three-fourths of the students surveyed took the help of private tutors or attended coaching centres". 7,225 respondents in 26 upazilas of 16 districts across 8 divisions plus 5 city corporations, covering **Class V to IX** — [The Business Standard, 30 Mar 2024](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/education/cost-education-jumps-51-secondary-25-primary-students-study-819286)
- Later coverage names it "CAMPE Education Watch 2023" — [Dhaka Tribune, 4 Nov 2026](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/education/423686/low-income-children%E2%80%99s-schooling-at-risk-as). Prothom Alo calls it CAMPE's March 2024 report *School Education in Bangladesh: Sustainable Post-Pandemic Recovery* — [Prothom Alo English, updated 6 Oct 2025](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/pvg3rbvka0)
- The previous round, Education Watch 2022 ("Post-Pandemic Education: Recovery and Renewal of School Education"), found that about **85% of Class 8 and 9 students took private tuition** to recover from Covid learning losses — [The Daily Star, 9 Sep 2023](https://www.thedailystar.net/node/3414601)

**Older and other prevalence estimates**
- UNESCO GEM South Asia 2022: the share of households paying for private tutoring in Bangladesh rose from 28% to 54% in rural areas and from 48% to 67% in urban areas between 2000 and 2010. This is old, household-survey-based data — [World Education Blog (UNESCO GEM), 2 Nov 2022](https://world-education-blog.org/2022/11/02/private-education-has-grown-faster-in-south-asia-than-any-other-region-in-the-world/)
- Other figures in circulation: about 75% of secondary and 35% of primary students get some tutoring (Alam & Zhu 2022), and "92% of students" took tutoring in 2017 (UNESCO 2018, all levels) (snippet) — [Frontiers in Psychology 2022 / UMN dissertation, via search results](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.922743/full)

**Tutor and coaching fees**
- Education Watch 2022, parent-reported: **about 64% of Class 8 and 50% of Class 9 students spent Tk 1,100–3,000 per month** on private tuition — [The Daily Star, 9 Sep 2023](https://www.thedailystar.net/node/3414601)
- Dhaka coaching centre, Motijheel: an official said **Tk 5,000–8,000** depending on class and subjects, for classes 4–5 days a week. A Class 9 student pays Tk 4,000 for coaching in four subjects. Parents spend at least Tk 5,000–6,000 a month on private lessons. One parent of two spends Tk 10,000–14,000 a month on coaching plus tuition. Quote: "When almost everyone is taking coaching, it becomes difficult to keep your child out of it" — [Dhaka Tribune, 23 Aug 2026](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/education/418170/why-coaching-remains-a-%E2%80%98second-shift%E2%80%99-for)
- A Dhaka Collegiate School student pays about **Tk 6,500 a month for two home tutors** — [Prothom Alo English, updated 6 Oct 2025](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/pvg3rbvka0)
- A low-income Dhaka household (domestic worker earning about Tk 9,000 a month) pays **Tk 700 a month** in tuition for a Class 7 daughter. Total education spend is about Tk 11,000–12,000 a year — [Dhaka Tribune, 4 Nov 2026](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/education/423686/low-income-children%E2%80%99s-schooling-at-risk-as)
- A Bashundhara (Dhaka) parent pays Tk 5,000 a month for private tutoring for a Class 5 child — [Prothom Alo English, 13 Jul 2025](https://en.prothomalo.com/youth/education/sijg8pi2w7)
- School-run "additional classes" may charge at most Tk 300 per subject per month under government rules. One parent reported a school's coaching charging Tk 1,500 for English and Tk 1,000 for each other subject (snippet) — [bdnews24, via search results](https://bdnews24.com/education/6fng7hl0ii)
- Elite or English-medium tutoring: Tk 8,000–10,000 in Dhanmondi and Tk 15,000–18,000 in Gulshan. The unit (per month or per subject) is unclear (snippet) — [The Business Standard, Dec 2025](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1315626)
- Classified listings in Dhaka (Bikroy): Class 6–10 science tutor Tk 8,000 "per service, negotiable", and Class 9–10 English/maths Tk 4,000. Units are unclear (snippet) — [Bikroy listings](https://bikroy.com/mogbazar/child-care-and-education-services/abhijny-hom-ttiuttr-buet-postgraduate-sust-graduate-aaDa5yHdnPLDxcPO9vNBmLun.html)
- Reasons parents give for coaching: irregular classes, large classes with little individual attention, exam fear, peer pressure — [Dhaka Tribune, 23 Aug 2026](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/education/418170/why-coaching-remains-a-%E2%80%98second-shift%E2%80%99-for)
- Supply side: government secondary schools have about 3,500 vacant teaching posts (about 20%) and an average student-teacher ratio of 1:37. Schools were closed on 279 of 427 days between Aug 2023 and Sep 2024 (CAMPE research) — [Prothom Alo English, updated 6 Oct 2025](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/pvg3rbvka0)
- Ministry of Education (2016): the 2012 curriculum created a **Tk 300 billion market for coaching and guidebooks** — [bdnews24, 18 Jan 2025](https://bdnews24.com/bangladesh/a697f62dd3d9)

**Where the 71% comes from**
- "Households account for the largest share of total education spending in South Asia (38%)… particularly high in Nepal (50%), Pakistan (57%) and Bangladesh (71%)". From the GEM 2022 South Asia report *Non-state actors in education: Who chooses? Who loses?*, released 2 Nov 2022 — [World Education Blog (UNESCO GEM), 2 Nov 2022](https://world-education-blog.org/2022/11/02/private-education-has-grown-faster-in-south-asia-than-any-other-region-in-the-world/)
- Dhaka launch with BRAC: households 71%, government 29%. Private/NGO school fees are about 3 times government school fees. 94% of secondary students attend private institutions. Bangladesh spends under 2.5% of GDP on education — [The Financial Express, 4 Jan 2023](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/national/households-bear-71pc-cost-of-education-government-shares-rest-unesco-1672756273)
- The borrowing figure is easy to misquote. The UNESCO blog says "around a third of families borrowed money for their children to study at **private polytechnics**". The FE article widened this to one-third of households borrowing for "private education" — [World Education Blog, 2 Nov 2022](https://world-education-blog.org/2022/11/02/private-education-has-grown-faster-in-south-asia-than-any-other-region-in-the-world/); [The Financial Express, 4 Jan 2023](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/national/households-bear-71pc-cost-of-education-government-shares-rest-unesco-1672756273)
- Some outlets attribute the figure to the "2021/2 GEM report". A Dhaka Tribune piece on the 2021/2 global report says "almost two-thirds" of education costs fall on households (snippet) — [Dhaka Tribune, via search results](https://www.dhakatribune.com/world/317243/unesco-7%25-of-families-in-bangladesh-have-to)

### Inferences
- The correct citation for 76.5% is: Education Watch 2023 (CAMPE), reported by The Daily Star on 30 Mar 2024. It covers Classes 5–9 in 26 upazilas plus city corporations. It is not a national statistical survey, and it does not cover Class 10 directly. One account (TBS) defines it as "private tutors **or** coaching centres". So "have a private tutor" may overstate how narrow the measure is.
- The correct citation for 71% is: UNESCO GEM Report 2022 South Asia edition (2 Nov 2022), not the global 2021/2 report. The underlying household data is survey-based and likely years old, so treat it as an order-of-magnitude figure.
- A realistic spending band for a Class 8–10 student's tutoring is about Tk 1,100–3,000 a month nationally, and Tk 4,000–8,000 a month in Dhaka for multiple subjects or coaching. Against this, SenseBook's Tk 399 a month is about 13–36% of a typical national tutor spend and 5–10% of a Dhaka spend. The real question is whether parents see it as a substitute or as an add-on, since peer pressure keeps coaching "non-optional".

### Gaps
- No HIES 2022 or World Bank figure for the share of secondary students taking tutoring was found. HIES 2022 tables on tutoring (if they exist) were not reached.
- No systematic, dated survey of tutor fees by class and urban/rural for 2025–2026 was found. Rural per-subject rates are missing. Listings and anecdotes are not representative.
- The full Education Watch 2023 report PDF was not opened. Its exact tutoring definition and its cost breakdown (tutor vs coaching vs guidebooks) remain unverified.

## 2. Household spending on education (HIES 2022 and Education Watch)

### Takeaway
HIES 2022 puts average household education spending at only about Tk 573–578 a month, about 1.8–1.9% of household spending. This is an average over all households, including those without students. Per student, HIES gives Tk 1,745 (urban Tk 2,927, rural Tk 1,171), but the period is not stated. Education Watch puts out-of-pocket spending per secondary student at Tk 27,340 a year (2022), rising to about Tk 41,424 a year (2023, extrapolated from the first half). The urban secondary figure is about Tk 35,662. No quintile breakdown was found.

### Cited Findings
- HIES 2022 (BBS, 14,400 households): average monthly household income **Tk 32,422**, expenditure **Tk 31,500**. Education **Tk 573 a month**, ranked 10th of 13 categories. Food is about 42% (Tk 13,193), health Tk 2,115 (6.7%) — [Prothom Alo English, updated 20 Dec 2023](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/xjj8jgjvnj)
- HIES 2022: monthly household education spend **Tk 578**, down from Tk 836 in 2016-17. Share of spending **1.89%**, down from 5.42%. Spending per student **Tk 1,745**, up 93.89% from Tk 900 in 2016-17: **rural Tk 1,171, urban Tk 2,927**. Households spend Tk 3.66 on health for every Tk 1 on education. Private-school share of students 7.65% — [The Business Standard, 6 Jan 2024](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/health/tough-choice-health-beats-education-household-expenses-770110)
- The same per-student figures are repeated in a feature — [The Business Standard, 15 Jan 2024](https://www.tbsnews.net/features/panorama/when-basic-necessities-become-your-biggest-expenditure-775170)
- Education Watch 2023: average annual cost for a **Class 9 student Tk 27,340 (2022)**, and **Tk 20,712 in the first six months of 2023**. Class 5: Tk 13,882 (2022) and Tk 8,647 (H1 2023). Out-of-pocket costs rose **51% at secondary** and 25% at primary — [The Daily Star, 30 Mar 2024](https://www.thedailystar.net/node/3578376)
- Education Watch 2023, full-year 2023: average annual family cost per child **Tk 41,424 at secondary** and Tk 17,294 at primary. Costs are "about a quarter less for rural areas". Private tutoring and commercial guidebooks were "major contributors" — [The Daily Star (op-ed, Manzoor Ahmed), 17 Apr 2024](https://thedailystar.net/node/3588381)
- Urban breakdown: **urban secondary student Tk 35,662 (2022)** and urban primary Tk 18,132 — [Dhaka Tribune, 4 Nov 2026](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/education/423686/low-income-children%E2%80%99s-schooling-at-risk-as). TBS pairs Tk 35,662 (urban) with **Tk 22,909 (rural)** "two years ago" without naming the level — [The Business Standard, 30 Mar 2024](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/education/cost-education-jumps-51-secondary-25-primary-students-study-819286)
- Main cost drivers, according to CAMPE's deputy director: private tutors' fees and notes/guidebooks (snippet) — [The Business Standard, 30 Mar 2024](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/819286)
- Fee caps under the Private Educational Institutions' Tuition Fee Policy 2024 (MPO schools, Classes 6–10, non-tuition fees per year): city corporations up to Tk 2,465, district/upazila towns Tk 1,850, rural Tk 1,405 — [Prothom Alo English, 13 Jul 2025](https://en.prothomalo.com/youth/education/sijg8pi2w7)
- Context: September 2026 inflation 8.34%. Wage growth 7.9%, below inflation for 56 consecutive months (BBS) — [Dhaka Tribune, 4 Nov 2026](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/education/423686/low-income-children%E2%80%99s-schooling-at-risk-as)

### Inferences
- Tk 20,712 × 2 = Tk 41,424 and Tk 8,647 × 2 = Tk 17,294. So the "2023 annual" figures are the first half doubled, and 41,424 / 27,340 is the "51% rise". They are extrapolations, not full-year measurements.
- HIES (Tk 578 per household per month, about Tk 6,900 a year) and Education Watch (Tk 27,340–41,424 per secondary student a year) differ by roughly 4–6 times. Likely reasons: HIES averages over all households (many have no student) and probably under-captures tutoring. Education Watch samples school students directly. Use Education Watch for the secondary-family pitch, and cite HIES only with that caveat.
- Affordability: Tk 14,990 is about **46% of one month's average household income** (Tk 32,422, HIES 2022). It is about **55% of a year's out-of-pocket spend per secondary student** (Tk 27,340, 2022), or about 36% of the 2023 extrapolation. First-year cost (device + 12 × Tk 399) is Tk 19,778.
- The 1.89% share (TBS) is consistent with Tk 578 / Tk 30,603 (a total-expenditure figure seen in some coverage). The 1.82% share is consistent with Tk 573 / Tk 31,500. The two outlets likely used different BBS tables.

### Gaps
- No HIES 2022 education spending by quintile or decile was found, and no HIES urban/rural household-level education totals. The BBS final report tables were not reached (one BBS PDF failed to load).
- The period for the HIES per-student figure (Tk 1,745: monthly or annual) is not stated in coverage.
- A claim that education fell to "2.78% of spending in 2022 from 5.68% in 2010" appeared in a search summary but could not be verified. Do not use it.

## 3. Guidebooks and notebooks (note-boi)

### Takeaway
Guidebooks are almost universal: **93% of secondary students used commercial guidebooks in 2023**, up from 82.5% in 2022. Parents reported spending an average of **Tk 2,065 per month on guides and notebooks** at secondary level (2022). Individual Class 9 guides cost about Tk 320–430 each. Guidebooks are banned below Grade 9 under a 1980 law, but they are sold openly.

### Cited Findings
- Guidebook use: secondary **82.5% (2022) and 93% (2023)**; primary 79% and 92% — [The Daily Star, 30 Mar 2024](https://www.thedailystar.net/node/3578376); also [The Business Standard, 30 Mar 2024](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/education/cost-education-jumps-51-secondary-25-primary-students-study-819286)
- Education Watch 2022: parents spent an average of **Tk 2,065 (secondary) and Tk 669 (primary) "last month"** on guidebooks and notebooks — [The Daily Star, 9 Sep 2023](https://www.thedailystar.net/node/3414601)
- Nilkhet booksellers: Panjeree Class 9 Mathematics guide **Tk 430**, English Tk 360, History and Social Science Tk 320. Traders estimate students in Classes 1–9 spend Tk 500–700 a year on guides. The article date is not confirmed, probably 2022 (snippet) — [The Daily Star, "Now school supplies get pricier"](https://tds-images.thedailystar.net/youth/education/news/now-school-supplies-get-pricier-too-3113691)
- A low-income family spent Tk 1,500 on guidebooks for Class 7, plus Tk 550 on notebooks and pens. A Mirpur private-school family budgets Tk 2,000 for guidebooks — [Dhaka Tribune, 4 Nov 2026](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/education/423686/low-income-children%E2%80%99s-schooling-at-risk-as)
- Notebook prices: 128-page notebook at least Tk 50–75 (was about Tk 30 four to five years ago). 200-page Tk 80–90. 300-page Tk 80–120. Books that cost Tk 200 now sell for Tk 350–400 — [Prothom Alo English, 13 Jul 2025](https://en.prothomalo.com/youth/education/sijg8pi2w7)
- The Note-Books (Prohibition) Act 1980 prohibits guidebooks for students below Grade 9. Publishers argue the law is outdated (snippet) — [Business Post BD, via search results](https://businesspostbd.com/national/banned-guidebooks-continue-to-dominate-education-system)

### Inferences
- Guidebooks are the habit SenseBook competes with most directly. They give ready answers for NCTB textbook exercises at Tk 300–450 a subject. A device that "explains" the textbook needs to show it is better than a ready-made answer key for exam-focused parents.
- Annual guidebook spend for a secondary student, about Tk 1,500–3,000 for a set, is well below SenseBook's first-year cost.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 dated price list for Class 9–10 guidebooks (Panjeree, Lecture etc.) was found. The Tk 2,065 "last month" figure may include notebooks and may be seasonal (start-of-year buying).

## 4. EdTech in Bangladesh: 10 Minute School, Shikho, others

### Takeaway
The two big platforms both cover NCTB Classes 6–10 and both now advertise AI help. 10 Minute School offers a full-year live "Online Batch" (Classes 6–10, 1 hour a day, 5 days a week, AI Super Solve). Its prices are only shown inside the dynamic store, though a bundle with Spoken English is listed at Tk 30,000. Shikho claims 3M+ students, launched a Bangla "Shikho AI" doubt-solver beta in Feb 2025, and in Dec 2025 launched **EduTab**, a Walton-made tablet with Shikho preloaded. EduTab is the closest hardware competitor to SenseBook, and its price has not been disclosed.

### Cited Findings
- 10 Minute School Online Batch 2026: Classes 6–10, all subjects (Bangla, English, Math, Science; group subjects for 9–10). Live classes 5 days a week, 1 hour each (6:30–7:30 PM or 8–9 PM). Daily MCQ exams, monthly major exam, model tests, monthly report cards, parent-teacher meetings, a mentor, at most 120 students per batch. **"AI Super Solve"** for doubts at any time. Bundle with Spoken English Junior listed at **Tk 30,000**; Spoken English Junior alone at Tk 28,000. The page claims 11,000+ Online Batch 2025 students "ranked first in class" — [10minuteschool.com product page, fetched 27 Nov 2026](https://10minuteschool.com/en/product/online-batch-2026-and-spoken-english-bundle)
- 10MS free courses for 2026 include a Class 6 Super Course and a Class 10 pre-test preparation course — [10minuteschool.com, fetched 27 Nov 2026](https://10minuteschool.com/en/product/free-class-6-super-course-2026)
- 10MS's 2023 batch had about 22 live classes a month for Classes 6–8 and 40 for Classes 9–10 (snippet) — [Dhaka Tribune, 2023](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/education/336436/10-minute-school-inaugurates-new-online-batch-for)
- 10MS background: founded 2015 by Ayman Sadiq; Sequoia-backed; $5.5M pre-Series A in 2023 (snippet, secondary source) — [Wikipedia: 10 Minute School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_Minute_School)
- Shikho claims **3 million+ students, 4.5 million+ app downloads** and 500,000+ learning materials. It offers live and recorded classes, animated lessons, MCQ tests, notes and report cards. No prices on the homepage — [shikho.com, fetched 27 Nov 2026](https://shikho.com)
- Early Shikho pilot price: Tk 100 a month for Grade 9–10 maths (old, likely around 2020–21) — [The Business Standard](https://tbsnews.net/feature/panorama/shikho-reshaping-education-technology-167410)
- Shikho AI beta (Feb 2025): helps Bangla-speaking students with academic doubts using curriculum-specific data, with educators reviewing outputs (snippet) — [The Daily Star, via search results](https://tds-images.thedailystar.net/tags/shikho-ai)
- Shikho and Meta "Think AI" free Bangla AI-literacy course (2025–26; dates conflict across sources) (snippet) — [The Daily Star press release](https://tds-images.thedailystar.net/news/tech-startup/news/shikho-launches-think-ai-meta-and-lightcastle-4165986)
- Startup Bangladesh (the ICT Division VC arm) invested in Shikho in May 2025 for "AI-powered learning" (snippet) — [startupbangladesh.vc](https://www.startupbangladesh.vc/startup-bangladesh-shikho)
- **EduTab** (Shikho + Walpad/Walton), launched about 1 Dec 2025: a standard Android tablet with full app-store access (not locked down), Shikho preloaded (animated lessons, practice, live classes, "AI support"). Sold direct to consumers at an "affordable price" (no figure) — [Dhaka Tribune, 1 Dec 2025](https://www.dhakatribune.com/business/397749/shikho-walpad-launch-edutab)
- EduTab specs: Android 16, Helio G99, 8.68-inch 1340×800 screen, 4GB/128GB, 6,000 mAh, 4G and Wi-Fi, 13MP and 8MP cameras. Price not stated — [The Business Standard, 21 Jan 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/corporates/walton-shikho-launch-learning-focused-tablet-edutab-1340111)
- Shikho content is "refined for Classes 6 through 12". Series A of $6.3M (2023). Analysts' risk: open Android may pull students to entertainment — [Future Startup, 2 Dec 2025](https://futurestartup.com/2025/12/02/shikho-and-walpad-launch-edutab/)

### Inferences
- The AI-tutor-in-Bangla space is no longer empty. Both leading platforms now advertise AI doubt-solving tied to the national curriculum. SenseBook's difference has to be **screen-free, the physical book, audio-first, and parent-facing reminders and spaced repetition**, not "AI in Bangla" alone.
- EduTab uses the same channel (Walton retail, parents) and the same pitch (curriculum + AI). Its price is unknown but likely near Walpad prices (Tk 16,000–21,000, see section 8). It sits right next to SenseBook's Tk 14,990.
- Rough arithmetic on the 10MS bundle: Tk 30,000 total, Spoken English Junior Tk 28,000 with Tk 7,000 off when Online Batch is bought. This hints that Online Batch alone is about Tk 9,000 a year (about Tk 750 a month). That number was not shown anywhere, so it is unverified.

### Gaps
- 10MS Online Batch 2026 and Shikho subscription prices for Classes 6–10 are not visible without the app or store (dynamic pages). Only the bundle price was found.
- No verified active-user or paying-user numbers for either platform in 2025–2026 (only self-claims).
- No evidence on how well either platform's AI works in Bangla. Other platforms (e.g., Bohubrihi, Ghoori) were not researched.

## 5. Government and NCTB resources

### Takeaway
All 2026 NCTB textbooks (647 titles, Bangla and English versions) are free online as PDFs at nctb.gov.bd, released 28 Dec 2025. Government platforms exist (Konnect for ages 13–18, Muktopaath, the Teachers' Portal / Shikkhok Batayon). DAISY "multimedia talking book" versions of textbooks exist for visually impaired students. Pandemic-era TV and video lessons ("Amar Ghore Amar School") were made for Classes 6–10, but their current status is unclear.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 textbooks online: **647 textbooks in both Bangla and English** at primary and secondary level on www.nctb.gov.bd, unveiled 28 Dec 2025 at the NCTB auditorium so students can read before the school year starts. The article does not mention audio or video versions — [Dhaka Tribune, 28 Dec 2025](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/399732/online-versions-of-2026-textbooks-published-on)
- Accessible talking textbooks: a2i, YPSA and partners produced DAISY multimedia talking books of NCTB textbooks. Coverage is given as Classes 1–10 or 1–12 (sources conflict). The NCTB chairman said they would be on the NCTB website for free download, with government-funded distribution from 2023. Bangladesh joined the Marrakesh Treaty on 26 Sep 2022 (snippet) — [a2i, 12 Jul 2023](https://a2i.gov.bd/?p=4709); [WIPO ABC, 2023](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/abc/w/news/2023/abc-partner-profiles-bangladesh-s-vashkar-bhattacharjee-and-the-young-power-in-social-action)
- Konnect: a2i "edutainment platform" for youth aged 13–18. Registered-user claims conflict: 270,000 (a2i 2023 brief), 2.7 million (Financial Express), 3 million+ (a2i page) (snippet) — [a2i Konnect Innovation Brief 2023](https://a2i.gov.bd/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Konnect-Innovation-Brief_2023.pdf); [The Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/education/smart-citizens)
- Muktopaath (government e-learning): over 400,000 teachers (snippet) — [EdTech Hub](https://edtechhub.org/?p=66807); about 1.9 million users a year (snippet) — [DPI Global](https://www.dpi.global/globaldpi/bangladesh_muktopaath)
- Teachers' Portal (Shikkhok Batayon): 650,000+ active users a month. The vendor says it won the UNESCO King Hamad ICT in Education Prize in 2025 (vendor claim, snippet) — [Riseup Labs](https://riseuplabs.com/teachers-portal-connects-over-650000-educators-nationwide/)
- Pandemic lessons: DSHE's "Amar Ghore Amar School" recorded lessons for Classes 6–10 aired on Sangsad TV from March 2020 and were uploaded to YouTube and Facebook (Konnect, Kishore Batayon, Ghore Boshe Shikhi channels). Parents found them scattered and hard to locate — [The Business Standard, 2020](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/education/secondary-level-class-lessons-go-air-tomorrow-59887); [The Daily Star, 2020](https://online-d11.thedailystar.net/city/news/televised-classes-schoolgoers-one-month-lot-left-unfulfilled-1899529)

### Inferences
- Free official PDFs of every NCTB book, in both languages, give SenseBook a clean source of textbook text. The same PDFs also let any phone AI tool (ChatGPT, Gemini, NotebookLM) work with NCTB content at no cost, which lowers SenseBook's content advantage.
- No official audio narration of mainstream textbooks for sighted students was found. The DAISY books are the nearest precedent, aimed at visually impaired students.

### Gaps
- It is not confirmed whether 2026 DAISY talking books were released, or whether any official video lessons for Classes 6–10 are currently maintained.
- No usage figures for students reading NCTB PDFs online were found.

## 8. Device access: smartphones, teens' phone access, tablet prices, phone-distraction concerns

### Takeaway
Smartphones are in **about 3 in 4 households (75.1%, Apr–Jun 2026)**, but only **about 2 in 3 people aged 5+ own any phone (65.6%)** and **59.2% use the internet**. The urban-rural internet gap is large (75.7% vs 43.6%, FY2024-25). Among secondary students, **58% have internet at home and 46% use it for study** (2023). There is no recent national data on teens owning their own phone; most children use a family phone. Entry Android tablets cost **about Tk 16,500–18,000** (Walton). Worry about phone addiction is widespread, but evidence for school students is thin.

### Cited Findings

## 9. Power cuts (load-shedding) and internet reliability, 2025–2026

### Takeaway
2026 has seen severe load-shedding. During the April 2026 SSC exams, many areas, especially rural ones, had **8–10 hours of outages a day**, often at night when students study, and students fell back on candles and kerosene lamps. Summer 2026 shortfalls reportedly passed 3,500 MW. Rural areas take most of the cuts while Dhaka stays relatively stable. No data on internet outage frequency was found.

### Cited Findings
- SSC 2026 (about 1,857,344 candidates, 3,885 centres) began amid load-shedding of **8 to 10 hours a day in many areas**, with rural areas worse (Chittagong, Barisal, Mymensingh, Sylhet, Rangpur, Khulna). National shortfall was 1,219 MW at 1 PM on the Monday and 1,051 MW at the 7 PM Sunday peak. A Banshkhali parent said students study by **candlelight or kerosene lamps**, and night-time cuts are the main problem. Officials linked the shortage to a global energy crisis — [Dhaka Tribune, 20 Apr 2026](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/education/408243/ssc-exams-begin-amid-severe-load-shedding)
- Student quote: "We don't even get four hours of electricity in a whole day". Main study hours are evening to 11 PM (snippet) — [bdnews24, 24 Apr 2026](https://bdnews24.com/bangladesh/163b384c84cb)
- Daily load-shedding exceeded **3,500 MW** at the height of the shortage in July–August 2026. Rural Bangladesh absorbed most of it while Dhaka stayed comparatively stable (snippet) — [ISAS, NUS](https://www.isas.nus.edu.sg/papers/bangladeshs-power-crisis-energy-vulnerability-and-political-consequences/); [Prothom Alo English](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/bangladesh-in-world-media/dugjvkf35s)
- A letter-writer asked authorities to stop load-shedding during public exams, saying evening outages hurt concentration (snippet) — [The Financial Express](https://today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/print/stop-load-shedding-during-public-exams-1776782588)
- Household internet access was only 58.2% in Apr–Jun 2026, down from 59.7% a year earlier — [The Daily Star, 20 Sep 2026](https://thedailystar.net/news/technology/news/mobile-phone-use-reaches-896-bangladesh-bbs-4278021)

### Inferences
- A device for evening home study in rural Bangladesh in 2026 needs a **battery that lasts several evenings, works in low light** (audio-first helps; a camera reading a page by candlelight may struggle), and **works offline** when mobile towers or home Wi-Fi go down during outages.
- Load-shedding is a selling point for audio-first, battery-powered design, but also a reliability risk for any cloud-dependent AI.

### Gaps
- No 2025 (as opposed to 2026) figures on outage hours were found, and nothing on how often mobile internet drops during load-shedding.
- No measurement was found of study hours lost to power cuts.
