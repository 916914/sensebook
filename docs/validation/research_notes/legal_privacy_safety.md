## 1. NCTB textbook rights, copyright exceptions (disability, education) and the Marrakesh Treaty

### Takeaway
Bangladesh law vests copyright in NCTB textbooks in the state. The 1983 NCTB Ordinance gave it to the Board, and the Copyright Act 2023 makes the Government the first owner of "government works" for 60 years from publication. The Note-Books (Prohibition) Act 1980 separately bans unauthorised "explanations… answers or solutions… translations or paraphrases" of textbooks. That matters more for SenseBook than copyright does, because SenseBook stores textbook text and audio and generates explanations and quizzes. The only clear statutory exception is s.70(2) of the Copyright Act 2023, for visually impaired readers. It is strictly non-profit and at production cost, so it does not cover a commercially sold device. No public NCTB licence, MoU or permission scheme for edtech firms was found. Bangladesh acceded to Marrakesh on 26 Sep 2022 (in force 26 Dec 2022), but domestic implementation is described as narrow and lagging.

### Cited Findings
**Who holds copyright**
- NCTB Ordinance 1983 (Ordinance No. LVII of 1983), s.15(2): "The copyright of all text books and other books published by the Board shall vest in the Board." s.15(1): only Board-approved or Board-published books may be prescribed as school textbooks. s.12: the Board's fund includes "fees and royalties collected by the Board". — [WIPO Lex, NCTB Ordinance 1983 entry (undated page)](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/7413)
- The 1983 Ordinance was repealed and replaced by the National Curriculum and Textbook Board Act, 2018 (Act No. 62 of 2018, 14 Nov 2018). — [bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd, act-1275 (search snippet)](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1275.html). I could not read the 2018 Act's text: the page's Bangla rendered as corrupted characters and the "details" page displayed the 1983 Ordinance. So I could not confirm whether the 2018 Act keeps the "copyright vests in the Board" wording.
- Copyright Act 2023 (Act No. 34 of 2023) replaced the Copyright Act 2000. — [bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd, Copyright Act 2023 (search snippet)](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1452.html)
- Copyright Act 2023, s.15(2)(ঙ): unless otherwise agreed, the Government is the first owner of copyright in a "government work". s.2(49) defines a government work to include works made under the authority of government bodies and statutory government authorities. s.28: copyright in a government work lasts 60 years from first publication. — [bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd, Copyright Act 2023 text (Bangla, read 27 Nov 2026)](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1452.html)
- Exclusive rights under s.69 / s.2(7) include reproduction, translation, adaptation (s.2(4): transforming a work into another form) and sound-recording rights. — [bdlaws, Copyright Act 2023](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1452.html)
- Penalties: s.84, general infringement, up to 4 years' imprisonment and/or fine up to Tk 5 lakh (non-commercial: up to 6 months or Tk 50,000). s.100, digital works, up to 4 years and/or Tk 4 lakh. s.106, repeat offences, up to 5 years / Tk 5 lakh. s.82: network service providers must remove infringing material after written notice or may be liable. — [bdlaws, Copyright Act 2023](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1452.html)
- NCTB textbooks themselves have included third-party material. In Jan 2023 the Daily Star reported that allegations that a Class VII textbook copied National Geographic material were found true, and NCTB withdrew two Class 6–7 books amid plagiarism criticism. — [The Daily Star, 18 Jan 2023](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/news/class-vii-textbook-allegations-copying-natgeo-are-true-3223811?amp); [TBS (undated in snippet)](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/education/nctb-withdraws-two-textbooks-amid-criticism-583106)

**Exceptions (education, disability)**
- Copyright Act 2023, s.70(1): reproduction, adaptation, recording, broadcast, publication, fair use or translation "for purposes and under conditions set in the rules" does not infringe. The specific education, teaching, exam and research purposes are left to rules that were not in the text read. s.2(42) defines "fair use" (সদ্ব্যবহার) unusually, as an "innocent commercial use without permission that promotes freedom of expression". — [bdlaws, Copyright Act 2023](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1452.html)
- s.73(1), for broadcast and performer rights only: recordings made solely for teaching or research, and excerpts used for education or research, are not infringements. — [bdlaws, Copyright Act 2023](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1452.html)
- s.70(2), visually impaired: where a work's standard format is unusable by visually impaired persons, a person or body acting for them may make or import Braille "or another special format". Three conditions apply: copies go to visually impaired persons at production cost only, on an "entirely non-profit" (সম্পূর্ণ অলাভজনক) basis; they are used only by visually impaired persons; and steps are taken to prevent commercialisation. Audio and DAISY are not named. "Authorised entity" is not defined. — [bdlaws, Copyright Act 2023](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1452.html)
- s.51: the Copyright Board may issue compulsory licences for publications suitable for visually impaired readers. — [bdlaws, Copyright Act 2023](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1452.html)
- The Copyright Act 2023 has no text-and-data-mining or AI-training provision. s.69(2)(চ) treats unauthorised modification or deletion of digital data as possible infringement. — [bdlaws, Copyright Act 2023](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1452.html)
- Opinion: s.70(2) is "narrower than the treaty". It covers visual disability only, while Marrakesh covers a broader print-disabled group. Guidance is needed on eligible beneficiaries, authorised entities and cross-border exchange. Many books are converted to accessible formats "months after the relevant exam". — [The Daily Star (op-ed, Vashkar Bhattacharjee), 24 Nov 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/opinion/views/news/the-marrakesh-treaty-must-lead-better-access-books-4331571)

**Note-Books (Prohibition) Act 1980**
- s.2(b) defines a "notebook" as any printed book containing "notes, annotations, explanations, comments, references, answers or solutions to any question… or translation or paraphrases of any part of any text-book". s.3 prohibits printing, publishing, importing, selling, distributing and circulating such notebooks. — [The Daily Star analysis "Banning notebooks: legal framework and shortcomings" (search snippet; date not shown)](https://images.thedailystar.net/node/2995991)
- In NCTB v. AM Shamsuddin, 48 DLR (AD) 184, the Appellate Division held that the Act "does not completely debar publication": a notebook "may be published either by the Board itself or under the authority of it". It called a notebook "nothing more than a 'text book' put in a different but material form" and upheld the restriction as reasonable under Art. 39(2). — [Lawyers & Jurists (case extract, undated)](https://www.lawyersnjurists.com/article/note-book-prohibition-act-1980/)
- Commentary says the Act has "no clear-cut provisions of enforcement mechanisms". — [The Daily Star analysis (search snippet)](https://images.thedailystar.net/node/2995991)

**How NCTB distributes, and edtech precedents**
- NCTB publishes free PDF versions of its textbooks each year (647 Bangla and English titles for primary and secondary in one report). The older portal ebook.gov.bd launched in 2011. — [The Financial Express (search snippet; date not confirmed)](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/home/nctb-says-online-versions-of-textbooks-for-primary-secondary-levels-available-from-sunday); [Dhaka Tribune, 2026 textbooks online (search snippet)](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/399732/online-versions-of-2026-textbooks-published-on)
- The government Teachers' Portal warns that "hundreds" of third-party sites use NCTB book names. — [teachers.gov.bd (search snippet)](https://www.teachers.gov.bd/index.php/blog/details/851942)
- No NCTB agreement, MoU, licence or dispute with 10 Minute School or Shikho was found in English-language results. The nearest 10 Minute School item found is a telecom partnership: Robi and 10 Minute School produced a Bangla AI learning book. — [The Financial Express (2025, search snippet)](https://today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/print/robi-10-minute-school-team-up-to-expand-ai-based-learning-1758390501)
- Accessible-textbook precedent: a2i (government), with WIPO's Accessible Books Consortium and the DAISY Consortium, produced DAISY multimedia talking books. The NGO YPSA developed DAISY reading materials for Classes 1–10, handed to visually impaired students each year. NCTB partnered with a2i to supply multimedia talking books and Braille books by 1 January each year. — [IFLA conference paper (Bhattacharjee, 2019; search snippet)](https://library.ifla.org/id/eprint/2733/1/S22-2019-bhattacharjee-en.pdf); [a2i national portal page (search snippet)](https://a2i.portal.gov.bd/site/page/befa4db2-b91f-4cf2-b92e-4fcc971d45c7/-)

**Marrakesh Treaty status**
- Bangladesh deposited its instrument of accession on 26 Sep 2022, welcomed by WIPO DG Daren Tang. — [WIPO DG news, 26 Sep 2022](https://www.wipo.int/web/director-general/w/daren-tang/news/2022/news_0051); [TBS (search snippet)](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/corporates/bangladesh-joins-marrakesh-treaty-facilitate-people-disabilities-504914)
- It entered into force for Bangladesh on 26 Dec 2022. The Feb 2026 US–Bangladesh Agreement on Reciprocal Trade lists the treaty at Art. 1.14(e). — [The Daily Star op-ed, 24 Nov 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/opinion/views/news/the-marrakesh-treaty-must-lead-better-access-books-4331571)
- Bangladesh acceded directly (accession), not by signature plus ratification. Some outlets loosely call it "ratification". — [TBS commentary (search snippet)](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/508742)
- One-year and three-year anniversary coverage says implementation has lagged. — [TBS (search snippet)](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/764666); [The Daily Star (search snippet)](https://images.thedailystar.net/node/3503056)

### Inferences
- The rights holder to approach is NCTB, and/or the Government via the Ministry of Education. A private company storing full NCTB text and pre-generated audio, and selling a device built on it, is reproduction and adaptation of a government work still in copyright (60 years from publication). The free PDFs do not carry an open licence that I could find.
- The Note-Books Act is the more specific risk for generated explanations and quizzes. Its definition is aimed at "printed books", and how it applies to AI-generated spoken explanations has not been tested (no case found). The Appellate Division reading — notebooks may be published "by the Board itself or under the authority of it" — suggests NCTB authorisation is the route that removes the risk.
- s.70(2) could cover a non-profit or authorised-entity distribution of accessible versions to blind readers at cost. It would not cover a for-profit device sale, or sighted children. A blind-reader programme run through a non-profit or a2i/NCTB partner fits the exception better than retail.
- Because NCTB books contain some third-party material (e.g., the 2023 NatGeo case), a licence from NCTB may not cover every image or passage.

## 2. Children's data: Bangladesh data law status, children's rules, transfers, regulator, penalties, cyber law, voice recordings, and global benchmarks

### Takeaway
The Personal Data Protection Ordinance 2025 has been replaced. It is now the **Personal Data Protection Act, 2026 (Act No. 63 of 2026, dated 10 April 2026)**, deemed in force from 6 Nov 2025. Under it a child is anyone under 18 and needs parent or guardian consent. Biometric data — expressly including facial images and voiceprints — is "sensitive". Cross-border transfer is allowed on consent or contract grounds but tied to a government data-classification schedule and "suitable" destinations. The regulator is the new National Data Management Authority (National Data Management Act 2026, Act 80 of 2026). Fines are administrative, up to Tk 25–50 lakh, and the fine sections start only by later gazette notice (after 18 months). The Cyber Security Ordinance 2025 was also replaced by a 2026 Act, now facing further amendment. Separately, **Google's own terms forbid using Gemini (API and Vertex AI) in any service "directed towards or likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18"**. That is a direct conflict with SenseBook's plan.

### Cited Findings
**Status of the data law**
- The PDPO 2025 (Ordinance No. 61 of 2025) was gazetted 6 Nov 2025 together with the National Data Governance/Management Ordinance 2025. — [Sovereign Chambers of Law (SCL), 23 Apr 2026](https://bd-scl.com/insights/personal-data-protection-ordinance-2025-compliance.html); [TBS, Nov 2025 (search snippet)](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/govt-issues-gazettes-2-landmark-ordinances-data-protection-governance-1281356)
- A Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Ordinance 2026 was signed 5 Feb 2026. — [Securiti (vendor), 29 Jun 2026](https://securiti.ai/bangladesh-personal-data-protection-act-overview/); [bdlaws act-1616 (search snippet)](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1616.html)
- On 9 Apr 2026 the elected parliament passed 31 bills dealing with interim-government ordinances, including the Personal Data Protection Bill, 2026. — [The Financial Express, 9 Apr 2026](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/home/parliament-passes-31-bills-related-to-ig-era-ordinances)
- The official text is the Personal Data Protection Act, 2026, Act No. 63 of 2026, with "10 April 2026" under the act number. s.1(3): deemed in force from 6 Nov 2025, except s.23 (Chief Data Officer) and ss.31–35, which take effect on a gazette-notified date after 18 months from promulgation. The PDPO 2025 is repealed. — [bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd, PDPA 2026 (Bangla text, read 27 Nov 2026)](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1692.html); [bdlaws, PDPO 2025 page noting repeal (search snippet)](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1574.html)
- Territorial scope, s.1(2): covers processing outside Bangladesh tied to offering goods or services to, or monitoring, people in Bangladesh. — [bdlaws PDPA 2026 (search snippet of act-1692)](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1692.html)

**Children**
- s.2(19): a "child" is a person under 18, or any other age the government prescribes. — [bdlaws, PDPA 2026](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1692.html)
- s.9(1): a fiduciary may collect or process a child's data only with consent of a parent, legal guardian or authorised decision-maker, by a procedure set in regulations. s.9(2): processing must protect and not undermine the child's rights and interests. s.9(3): guardian consent lasts until the child turns 18 or gains capacity. — [bdlaws, PDPA 2026](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1692.html)
- Conflict: press and secondary write-ups of the 2025 Ordinance said it "prohibits profiling, behavioral tracking, or targeted advertising directed at children". — [Asia News Network (search snippet)](https://asianews.network/bangladeshs-personal-data-protection-ordinance-2025-key-takeaways/). In the 2026 Act text read, "profiling" is defined (s.2(13)) but no child-specific profiling or advertising ban appears. — [bdlaws, PDPA 2026](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1692.html)

**Sensitive data, voice, automated decisions**
- s.2(21): sensitive personal data includes genetic, biometric, health, religious or political beliefs, sexual orientation, criminal records and real-time geolocation. s.2(14): biometric data includes facial images, iris, fingerprints, **voiceprints** and gait. Children's data is not itself listed as sensitive. — [bdlaws, PDPA 2026](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1692.html)
- Sensitive data requires "specific consent". — [Securiti, 29 Jun 2026](https://securiti.ai/bangladesh-personal-data-protection-act-overview/)
- s.13(1)(গ): data subjects may withdraw consent to automated decisions. s.13(6): fiduciaries must inform before automated processing. s.11(3): the access right covers the logic and significance of automated decisions. The term "AI" does not appear. — [bdlaws, PDPA 2026](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1692.html)

**Cross-border transfer and localisation**
- s.29: the government may classify data as public, internal, confidential or restricted under a schedule (its contents were not visible in the text read). Transfer abroad is allowed where the data subject consents, where it is part of a contract for goods or services, or where it relates to the subject's business, **education**, travel or migration, with consent (s.29(3)). Transfers may go only to countries with suitable storage technology and facilities "as prescribed" (s.29(4)). Large-volume cross-border transfers of sensitive identifiable data must be notified to the Authority (s.29(6)). The government may levy fees on profits from using citizens' data (s.29(5)). No general store-in-Bangladesh mandate appears. — [bdlaws, PDPA 2026](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1692.html)
- Conflict: SCL (writing about the Ordinance) says "confidential" or "restricted" data must be stored in Bangladesh, and other data may go only to adequate jurisdictions still to be defined "by notification". — [SCL, 23 Apr 2026](https://bd-scl.com/insights/personal-data-protection-ordinance-2025-compliance.html)

**Regulator, registration, breach, penalties**
- Regulator: the National Data Management Authority, established under s.8 of the National Data Management Act, 2026 (Act No. 80 of 2026, dated 10 Apr 2026, deemed in force from 6 Nov 2025). It is attached to the Prime Minister's Office and has one Executive Chairman and six members. It can issue binding directions (s.27), award compensation (s.44) and fine up to Tk 25 lakh for breach of that Act (s.42(2)). Appeals go to the ICT Act s.68 tribunal (s.45). No controller registration or licensing regime and no explicit localisation rule were found in that Act. — [bdlaws, National Data Management Act 2026 (Bangla text)](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1709.html)
- PDPA: no general registration duty for data fiduciaries. "Significant data fiduciaries" are designated by regulation (s.2(5)) and must appoint a Chief Data Officer (s.23). Some classes face independent audits (s.21). — [bdlaws, PDPA 2026](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1692.html)
- Breach notification (s.20(1)): notify the Authority where a breach is likely to cause significant harm. The timeline is left to regulations; no hours or days are fixed. — [bdlaws, PDPA 2026](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1692.html)
- PDPA penalties are administrative only; no criminal offence provisions appear. Failing to honour data-subject rights: up to Tk 25 lakh (s.32(1)), up to Tk 50 lakh for significant fiduciaries (s.32(2)), with further fines for repeats (s.32(3)). Failing to ensure data security: up to Tk 25 lakh (s.33). The Authority may also award compensation (s.35), and officers can be fined (s.36). — [bdlaws, PDPA 2026](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1692.html)
- Conflict: SCL describes turnover-based penalties of 1–2% (2–5% for significant fiduciaries) and an 18-month compliance window to about May 2027. That describes the Ordinance, not the Act text. — [SCL, 23 Apr 2026](https://bd-scl.com/insights/personal-data-protection-ordinance-2025-compliance.html)

**Cyber Security Ordinance 2025 → 2026 Act**
- The Cyber Security Ordinance 2025 was repealed and replaced by the Cyber Security Bill/Act 2026, passed by parliament. Prothom Alo dates this 30 April. In July 2026 the government proposed amendments. A new s.26A would criminalise "rumours" and "disinformation" (up to 10 years and/or Tk 40 lakh). Amended s.25 covers content "including AI-generated material" meant to defame or degrade; where it targets a woman or a person under 18, the penalty is up to 10 years and/or Tk 40 lakh. — [Prothom Alo English, 30 Jul 2026](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/wbq9kji6z7)
- Amnesty dates the enactment to 10 Apr 2026 and criticises a draft "Cyber Protection (Amendment) Act, 2026" that would allow broad content removal and blocking. — [Amnesty International, Nov 2026 (search snippet)](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/11/bangladesh-proposed-changes-to-cyber-laws-risk-granting-authorities-excessive-power-to-police-online-content/). Sources disagree on the name ("Cyber Security" vs "Cyber Protection" Act) and on the April date. — [The Wire (search snippet)](https://m.thewire.in/article/media/bangladeshs-cyber-law-could-become-a-weapon-against-journalism)

**Cloud AI provider terms (Gemini)**
- Gemini API / AI Studio terms (effective 23 Mar 2026): "You must be 18 years of age or older to use the APIs". Apps "directed towards or is likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18" are prohibited. On unpaid tiers, Google may use prompts and responses to improve products, and human reviewers may read them. Paid tiers are not used for training, and prompts are logged "for a limited period". — [Google AI Studio, Gemini API Additional Terms, effective 23 Mar 2026](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms)
- Google Cloud Service Specific Terms s.20(d), which apply to Generative AI Services including Gemini on Vertex AI: "Customer will not, and will not allow End Users to, use a Generative AI Service as part of a website, Customer Application, or other online service that is directed towards or is likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18". s.20(f) allows immediate suspension for a suspected violation. s.18: Customer Data is not used to train models without permission. — [Google Cloud Service Specific Terms (section renumbering dated 18 Feb 2026)](https://cloud.google.com/terms/service-terms)

**Global benchmarks**
- US COPPA amended Rule: effective 23 Jun 2025; most operators had to comply by 22 Apr 2026. — [Federal Register, 22 Apr 2025](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule); [Foley Hoag, Jul 2026 (search snippet)](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/security-privacy-and-the-law/2026/july/the-amended-children-s-online-privacy-protection-act-%28coppa%29-regulations-what-your-business-needs/)
- The amended Rule codifies the FTC's 2017 audio-file policy. A child's voice recording may be collected without parental consent only to answer the child's specific request, with no other personal data, and must be deleted immediately afterwards; the notice must say so. A proposed inclusion of "data derived from voice… or facial data" was dropped. — [Hunton (search snippet)](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-publishes-final-coppa-rule-amendments); [Latham & Watkins, May 2025 (search snippet)](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2025/05/ftc-publishes-updates-to-coppa-rule)
- Enforcement example: Amazon paid a $25m civil penalty (31 May 2023). The FTC alleged Alexa kept children's voice recordings indefinitely by default and failed to honour parents' deletion requests. The order required deleting the data and barred using it to train algorithms. — [US DOJ press release, 31 May 2023](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/amazon-agrees-injunctive-relief-and-25-million-civil-penalty-alleged-violations-childrens); [FTC business blog, May 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2023/05/out-mouths-babes-ftc-says-amazon-kept-kids-alexa-voice-data-forever-even-after-parents-ordered)
- GDPR Art. 8(1): consent-based processing for information society services offered directly to a child is lawful from age 16; below that, parental consent is required; member states may lower the age to no less than 13. Art. 8(2): "reasonable efforts to verify… taking into consideration available technology". — [gdpr-info.eu, Art. 8](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-8-gdpr/)
- UK Age Appropriate Design Code, Standard 14 (connected toys and devices): "include effective tools to enable conformance to this code". The ICO notes that connected toys' data-collection potential "through functions like cameras and microphones, is considerable", and publishes a sample connected-toy DPIA. The code is statutory guidance under the DPA 2018. — [ICO, Standard 14 (search snippet; page returned 403)](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/14-connected-toys-and-devices/); [ICO connected toy DPIA (search snippet)](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/dpia-tools/connected-toy/)
- EU AI Act Annex III point 3: AI systems "intended to be used to evaluate learning outcomes", including where results steer a learner's progress, are high-risk. So is AI used for admission, for assessing the appropriate level of education, or for exam proctoring. — [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)

### Inferences
- In Bangladesh, every Class 6–10 child user is a "child" (under 18). SenseBook therefore needs verifiable guardian consent for all processing, not only for the optional learning memory. Page images may capture faces or hands, and voice questions may be treated as voiceprints, so both likely fall under "sensitive" biometric data needing specific consent. This holds even though raw voice audio sent for transcription is arguably not a "voiceprint". Whether it is one is untested.
- Sending page images and audio to Google abroad is likely lawful under s.29(3) on consent or "education" grounds. It still depends on the unpublished classification schedule and the "suitable destination" list, and large volumes of sensitive data must be notified to the Authority. This is unsettled until the regulations and schedule are published.
- Fines (ss.31–35) are not yet enforceable. The gazette date is to come after 18 months from April 2026, so roughly late 2027 or later — an estimate. The duties themselves are already deemed in force from 6 Nov 2025.
- The biggest practical blocker found is contractual, not statutory. Google's terms bar both Gemini API and Vertex AI generative services from under-18 audiences. Unless SenseBook gets a separate written arrangement (none found), it needs a different model provider whose terms allow children, or a deployment where the child never reaches the model directly — and even that probably fails "likely to be accessed by" individuals under 18. This needs checking against each provider's current terms.
- Benchmarks point to these design defaults: push-to-talk rather than always-listening; delete audio right after answering (COPPA audio exception); no retention by default; parent dashboard with deletion that actually works (the Amazon lesson); clear recording indicators and offline function (ICO Std 14). Quiz scoring that steers progression would be "high-risk" under the EU AI Act — not binding in Bangladesh, but a useful reference.

## 4. Battery safety: IEC 62133-2, UN 38.3, IEC 62368-1, air/courier shipping to Bangladesh, children's-device recalls, thermal limits

### Takeaway
A 2,000 mAh LiPo is roughly 7.4 Wh at 3.7 V nominal (an estimate). That puts it well inside the "small battery" rules, but it still needs a UN 38.3 test summary for any transport. IEC 62133-2 is the cell and pack safety standard, and IEC 62368-1 the end-product standard. Since 1 Jan 2026, batteries packed with equipment above 2.7 Wh must ship by air at 30% charge or less. Batteries installed in equipment are strongly recommended at 30% or less. Loose cells cannot fly on passenger aircraft. Some carriers refuse lithium batteries to Bangladesh, so cells should be sourced already installed or bought locally. For skin contact over 1 minute, IEC 62368-1's TS1 limit is 48 °C. A widely sold children's speaker (Yoto Mini) was recalled twice in 2024 for Li-ion overheating.

### Cited Findings
**Standards content**
- UN 38.3 has eight sequential tests (T1 altitude, T2 thermal, T3 vibration, T4 shock, T5 external short, T6 impact/crush, T7 overcharge, T8 forced discharge). T1–T5 run on the same samples, and T8 applies to cells only. — [Kiwa (test lab, search snippet)](https://www.kiwa.com/en/de/services/testing/battery-testing); [Intertek (search snippet)](https://intertek.com/batteries/un-38-3-testing)
- IEC 62133-2:2017 (2nd edition) covers portable sealed secondary lithium cells and batteries. It removed vibration, mechanical shock and low-pressure tests because UN/DOT 38.3 covers them. — [TÜV SÜD (search snippet)](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-us/industries/mobility-and-automotive/automotive-and-oem/automotive-testing-solutions/battery-testing/iec-62133)
- Cell, battery and equipment manufacturers and distributors (cells made after 30 Jun 2003) must make the UN 38.3 test summary available, per the UN Manual of Tests and Criteria Rev. 8, 38.3.5. A website plus QR code or URL is acceptable. — [IATA Battery Guidance Document, revised for 2026 regulations (dated 01/01/2026)](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/05e6d8742b0047259bf3a700bc9d42b9/lithium-battery-guidance-document.pdf)
- IEC 62368-1 sets touch-temperature limits based on ISO 13732-1. For TS1 accessible parts in contact for more than 1 minute (e.g., "continuous use of control elements such as handles"), the limit is 48 °C for metal and for plastic/rubber. TS2 is 10 K above TS1. The manufacturer determines the anticipated contact time. Under abnormal conditions not evident to the user, TS2 limits for under-1-second contact apply (e.g., 80 °C metal). — [UL technical brief "IEC 62368-1 Touch Temperature Limits" (UL Taiwan), 22 Oct 2010](https://japan.ul.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/27/2014/06/1_techbrief_touchtemp.pdf)
- In the 2nd edition the table is Table 38. IEC 62368-1 replaced IEC 60950-1 in Dec 2020. — [Advanced Energy, 27 Jul 2021](https://www.advancedenergy.com/en-us/about/news/blog/design-considerations-for-maximum-temperature-per-iec-safety-standards/). Limits are tested at a standard 25 °C ambient. — [TÜV Rheinland FAQ (search snippet)](https://www.tuv.com/landingpage/en/iec-62368-1/meta-navigation/faq/)

**Air and courier shipping**
- From 1 Jan 2026 (ICAO TI 2025-26, IATA DGR 67th ed.): Li-ion packed with equipment (PI 966) Section I must ship at 30% SoC or less. Section II cells or batteries over 2.7 Wh must also be at 30% SoC or less, otherwise they go under Section I with state approvals. For Li-ion contained in equipment (PI 967), 30% SoC or less (or 25% or less indicated capacity) is "strongly recommended" but not mandatory. — [IATA Battery Guidance Document 2026](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/05e6d8742b0047259bf3a700bc9d42b9/lithium-battery-guidance-document.pdf)
- Li-ion batteries shipped alone (PI 965) must be at 30% SoC or less and "cannot be shipped on a passenger aircraft" without approval (SP A201). PI 965 Section IA/IB packages need a Cargo Aircraft Only label. Section II thresholds: Li-ion cell 20 Wh or less, battery 100 Wh or less. — [IATA Battery Guidance Document 2026](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/05e6d8742b0047259bf3a700bc9d42b9/lithium-battery-guidance-document.pdf)
- Parcelforce (UK): "Lithium batteries cannot be sent to this country" (Bangladesh). — [Parcelforce Worldwide directory, Bangladesh (search snippet)](https://www.parcelforce.com/worldwide-directory/bangladesh). Lithium metal batteries are prohibited on Emirates flights that UPS uses to Bangladesh. — [VerykShip UPS restrictions list (search snippet)](https://www.verykship.com/ups/BD/PorhibitedAndRestrictedItems). DHL says not all destinations accept battery shipments. — [DHL (search snippet)](https://dhl.com/discover/en-sg/b2b-advice/country-guides/how-to-ship-batteries)

**Recalls and incidents (children's and comparable devices)**
- Yoto Mini children's speaker. CPSC recall No. 25-065, 12 Dec 2024, re-announcing an April 2024 recall. The Li-ion battery "can overheat and catch fire". About 251,165 US units plus about 18,932 in Canada, sold Nov 2021–Apr 2024 for about $70, made in China. Yoto received 9 US and 3 UK reports of overheating or melting, with no injuries. The remedy is a full battery replacement kit, even for owners who got the "smart cable" in the first recall. — [CPSC, 12 Dec 2024](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2025/Yoto-Reannounces-Recall-of-Yoto-Mini-Speakers-for-Children-Due-to-Burn-and-Fire-Hazards-New-Full-Battery-Replacement-Kit-Now-Available); first notice [CPSC, 11 Apr 2024 (search snippet)](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2024/Yoto-Recalls-Yoto-Mini-Speakers-for-Children-Due-to-Burn-and-Fire-Hazards); UK notice for the 2021–2023 edition [UK OPSS (search snippet)](https://www.gov.uk/product-safety-alerts-reports-recalls/product-safety-report-yoto-mini-2021-2023-edition-2412-0122)
- Fitbit Ionic smartwatch, an adult wearable held against the skin: recalled 2 Mar 2022 after at least 115 US overheating reports and 78 burn reports; Fitbit later paid a $12m civil penalty. — [LiveNOW from FOX (search snippet)](https://www.livenowfox.com/news/fitbit-pay-12m-fine-ionic-smartwatch-burns)
- HearMuffs children's headphones (2022): 19 reports of included AAA alkaline batteries rupturing; no injuries. Not Li-ion. — [CBS Boston (search snippet)](https://www.cbsnews.com/boston/news/childrens-hearmuffs-recall-rupturing-batteries-consumer-product-safety-commission/)
- CPSC's recent list (Nov 2026) shows lithium fire recalls mostly in power banks, e.g. HAIARA power banks, 5 Nov 2026, about 210,000 units. — [CPSC recalls search page (read 27 Nov 2026)](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls?combine=lithium%20children)

### Inferences
- 2,000 mAh × 3.7 V ≈ 7.4 Wh — an estimate assuming 3.7 V nominal; check the cell datasheet. This is under the 20 Wh cell / 100 Wh battery Section II limits and above the 2.7 Wh SoC threshold for PI 966.
- Practical import route: bring cells in installed in boards or assemblies (PI 967), or by sea, or from local stock with a UN 38.3 summary. Air courier of loose cells (PI 965) to Dhaka is likely to be refused or slow.
- The device is held against a book and in the hand for long reading sessions. Designing so that hand-contact surfaces stay at or below 48 °C, plastic, over 1 minute — and well below that, given child users and Dhaka's 30–35 °C ambient versus the 25 °C test ambient — is the relevant engineering target. Separating the cell from the SoC/heat source and adding a thermal cutoff is consistent with the standards' approach. This is untested for SenseBook.
- The Yoto case shows that "no injuries" Li-ion overheating reports in a children's product still led to two recalls and a full battery replacement programme. Recall cost and traceability (serial numbers, batch tracking of cells) should be planned from the start.

## 5. E-waste: Hazardous Waste (E-waste) Management Rules 2021 — producer obligations

### Takeaway
The 2021 Rules, published 10 Jun 2021 under the Environment Conservation Act 1995, make manufacturers, assemblers and importers of IT and communication equipment "producers". Producers must register with the Department of Environment, file a management plan, set up or join collection centres, label products with take-back information, restrict RoHS substances, and meet collection targets rising from 10% in year 1 to 50% from year 5. Enforcement is weak. TIB found in Dec 2025 that DoE had taken no visible steps to bring producers into registration and that there is no national inventory to measure targets.

### Cited Findings
- The Rules were published 10 Jun 2021 by the Department of Environment under the Environment Conservation Act 1995 and are in force on publication. Schedule categories include home appliances, monitoring and control equipment, medical equipment, automatic machines, and IT and communication equipment. — [Enviliance ASIA, 22 Jun 2021](https://enviliance.com/regions/south-asia/bd/report_2900)
- Producer and manufacturer obligations: register with DoE on a prescribed form with a WEEE management plan; obtain environmental clearance; establish individual or joint collection centres and set aside funds; meet collection targets of 10% (year 1), 20%, 30%, 40%, then 50% from year 5. Products, labels or consumer information must show seller details and the registered collection centre. Importers are named among those bound by the targets. The 10 EU RoHS substances are restricted. — [Enviliance, 22 Jun 2021](https://enviliance.com/regions/south-asia/bd/report_2900)
- Penalties run through s.15(1) of the 1995 Act: first offence up to 2 years and/or Tk 2 lakh; repeat offences 2–10 years and/or Tk 2–10 lakh. — [Enviliance, 22 Jun 2021](https://enviliance.com/regions/south-asia/bd/report_2900)
- Implementation, per TIB (policy brief 152/2025, research report published 30 Dec 2025):
  - Four years in, there is "no reliable nationwide inventory" to track targets.
  - DoE has taken no "visible, effective measures" to register producers, importers or distributors.
  - Of 14 e-waste enterprises registered with BTRC, half lack DoE registration.
  - 14,985 t of banned e-waste was imported in 2022–2024.
  - TIB recommends a separate EPR guideline with phased product categories and funding for Producer Responsibility Organisations.
  — [Transparency International Bangladesh, Policy Brief 152/2025, Dec 2025](https://ti-bangladesh.org/upload/files/policy-brief/2026/Policy-Brief-on-e-Waste-Management-152-En.pdf)
- About 78% of entities surveyed had no take-back system, and less than 10% of about 3.0 million tonnes of annual e-waste is formally recycled. — [Dhaka Tribune, reporting a VOICE assessment (search snippet)](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/bangladesh-environment/413913/voice-bangladesh-recycles-less-than-10%25-of)
- In 2022, electrical-goods companies asked the government to delay the Rules. — [Mongabay, Oct 2022](https://news.mongabay.com/2022/10/bangladesh-e-waste-rules-hang-in-limbo-as-electrical-goods-companies-ask-for-delay/)

### Inferences
- A Dhaka company that imports parts and assembles SenseBook is likely a "producer" (assembler and importer) of IT and communication equipment. On paper that means DoE registration, a take-back point, take-back labelling, RoHS-compliant parts and collection targets. Enforcement is weak in practice. A simple take-back and battery-return scheme is cheap insurance, fits the open-hardware story, and matters because a Li-ion cell is the main hazard at end of life.
- The "year 1" start date for targets (from the 2021 start or from each producer's registration) is ambiguous in the sources.

### Inferences
- There is no Bangladeshi AI-for-children rule to certify against. The defensible position is to map SenseBook's design to UNICEF 3.0's 10 requirements, plus the PDPA child and automated-decision clauses. Key elements: guardian-controlled memory, no companion or "friend" persona (UNICEF flags companions), human-readable explanations of why an answer was given, and testing in Bangla.
- If the draft AI Policy is adopted with an NCTB/MoE role in children's educational AI, it could create a de facto approval channel. That would favour a formal NCTB relationship, also needed for the copyright and Note-Books questions in Section 1.
