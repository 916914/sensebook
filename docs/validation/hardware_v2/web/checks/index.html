How each check works

  • Overlapping parts: every pair of parts whose boxes come within 0.3 mm is intersected as solids (OpenCascade); any shared volume above 0.02 mm³ counts. Pairs that touch by design (a window in its shell opening, a chip on its board, a flex in its connector) are listed in the build script and skipped.
  • Poses: v1's six poses (hardcover, paperback, tripod without a book, in the hand, stick extended, stowed) move each part group as v1 does, and the overlap check runs again.
  • Sight lines: a ray from each camera to every point of the page it reads best (3626 rays per option) is tested against every opaque part.
  • Resolution: each camera is a pinhole model with its sensor (8160 × 6144 px), field of view and barrel distortion. The dpi at a point is the smaller of the two directions, by central differences over 0.5 mm, on a 28 × 16 grid over the printed text (120 × 80 in the chart script).
  • Aims: every aim in 1° steps (30–65° down, 0–40° outward) with each module within 10.5° of the facet; the aim with the highest worst-case dpi wins, the flattest among near-equals.
  • Light: each LED is a beam aimed at its own patch of the page; a linear program finds the least light that keeps every point of the printed text between 300 and 750 lux.
  • Size: the bounding box of everything in the head is compared with v1's.
